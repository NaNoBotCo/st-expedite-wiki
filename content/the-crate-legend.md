Everywhere Expedite is venerated, the same origin story is told — and it is almost certainly not true.

## The story

A crate arrives at a convent. Stamped on the side is a word — *spedito*, *expédit*, *expedite*: Italian or French shipping-clerk's shorthand for "dispatched / sent." The nuns, finding an unlabelled saint's statue or relics inside, take the stamped word to be the holy name. And so a saint called **Expedite** is born from a delivery label.

## It is told in at least four places

*Legend —* The tale is **multiply localised**, each community claiming it as its own:

- **Paris, 1781** — a convent (often the Denfert-Rochereau community) receives the crate. This is the best-known version; the date is suspiciously precise.
- **New Orleans** — the Ursulines, or the Our Lady of Guadalupe chapel, receive a statue in a crate marked "Expedite" and cannot identify the saint; the *Times-Picayune* dates this telling to around **1921**.
- **Sicily / Independence, Louisiana** — a Sicilian-immigrant variant with its own chapel.
- **São Paulo / Haiti** — further retellings.

## Why scholars don't believe it

*Scholarship —* The legend fails on dates. Veneration of Expeditus in **Germany, Sicily and Turin predates every version of the crate story by decades or centuries** — you cannot invent a saint in 1781 Paris who already had churches in 17th-century Messina. The etymology runs the other way: the *cult* generated the pun, and the pun generated the legend, not the reverse.

*Inference —* There is even a literary vector for the story's spread: the German poet **Christian Morgenstern** published a satirical poem, "St. Expeditus," in *Palmström* (1910), retelling the crate tale as a joke — which helped fix it in popular memory as if it were history. Some French sources frame the whole legend as anticlerical satire.

## The 1781 coincidence worth savouring

The Paris legend is dated 1781. In that *same* year — **18 April 1781** — Expeditus was, verifiably, made the secondary patron of [Acireale](italy.html) in Sicily, patron of its merchants and navigators. The painting above hangs in Acireale's church of Gesù e Maria and dates to that event. So the year the saint is *legendarily* invented in a Paris convent is the same year he is *documentably* honoured by a Sicilian city that had venerated him for generations. History and legend, wearing the same date.
