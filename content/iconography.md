The standard image is one of the most legible in the whole calendar of saints, because it *is* its own explanation.

## The standard type

*Documented —* Expedite appears as a young **Roman legionary** in military dress. In his raised right hand he holds a **cross inscribed HODIE** — Latin for "today." In his other hand, the **palm of martyrdom**. Beneath his right foot he tramples a **crow (or raven)**, and from the bird's beak unfurls a banderole reading **CRAS** — "tomorrow."

*Tradition —* The gloss devotees give: the devil, appearing as a crow, tried to talk the new convert into postponing his baptism — *cras, cras*, "tomorrow, tomorrow," the very sound a crow makes. Expedite stamped the bird down and declared *Hodie!* — today. He is therefore the saint against putting things off.

## It was not always so — the image has a history

One of the tidier findings of this project is that the "standard" image is the *end* of an evolution, not the start:

- **Late 17th c. (Sicily):** the earliest church images, at Messina and then Acireale.
- **18th c. (Germany/Austria):** a distinct type shows the saint **pointing at a clock or sundial** — no crow at all. Speed, but not yet the pun.
- **Then:** clock **and** crow together.
- **Late 19th c.:** the mature **HODIE-cross + CRAS-crow** type, mass-diffused on cheap chromolithograph holy cards (the 1897 Milan card above is typical) that carried the devotion across the world.

*Contested —* A separate **Turin** tradition is said to have depicted a wholly different figure — an **elderly, white-bearded man** beside a clock, patron of merchants — which would predate everything else. It needs archival confirmation.

## Regional variation

*Practitioner —* On [Réunion](reunion.html) the image is subordinate to the **blood-red** of the shrine itself. In [New Orleans](new-orleans.html) practitioners read the statue's position *beside the church door* as scripture for where he belongs on a home altar — by the front or back door, the threshold saint.
