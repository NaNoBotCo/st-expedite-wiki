**Terminology caution used throughout:** *Voodoo* = the New Orleans religion (spirits/lwa, priesthood, congregational history); *hoodoo* = African American folk magic / rootwork, no congregation required. Sources blur these constantly — blurring is flagged per-section below. Per Wikipedia's Louisiana Voodoo article, historian Jeffrey Anderson calls the distinction "blurry" and dependent on who explains it; a 1940 practitioner put it as "Voodoo means the worker, hoodoo the things they do" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Voodoo) [documented]. Saint Expedite practice sits deliberately across Catholic devotion, hoodoo, and Voodoo — practitioners themselves do not always partition it, and this file records their usage as found.

## 1. Chapel & statue — Our Lady of Guadalupe Church / International Shrine of St. Jude

**The building** (411 N. Rampart St., edge of the French Quarter):
- Built **1827** as a **mortuary chapel** for yellow fever victims, sited near St. Louis Cemeteries #1 and #2 so funerals of the contagious dead would bypass St. Louis Cathedral; officials believed "exhalations from the dead could spread the disease" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe_Church_%26_International_Shrine_of_St._Jude) [documented]. Ghost-tour copy adds it was positioned "right in front of" St. Louis Cemetery for easy access to burials and mass graves (https://neworleansghosttour.com/our-lady-of-guadalupe/) [documented, tour-embellished framing].
- **1918**: Archbishop John Shaw invited the **Missionary Oblates of Mary Immaculate** to take over the disused chapel; they renamed it Our Lady of Guadalupe (Wikipedia, ibid.) [documented].
- **1930s**: answered prayers to St. Jude led to ongoing novenas and the erection of the **St. Jude shrine** (left of the altar, contains a St. Jude relic); hence "International Shrine of St. Jude" (Wikipedia, ibid.) [documented].
- Called the **oldest surviving church building in New Orleans** (never substantially rebuilt; St. Louis Cathedral is older as an institution but rebuilt) (https://www.nola.com/300/the-almost-true-legend-of-st-expedite-in-new-orleans/article_46709902-d3d3-504b-b501-c8d35b679e97.html) [documented].
- Context: in Nov 2025 the Archdiocese of New Orleans put 150+ parishes through Chapter 11 as part of an abuse settlement (Wikipedia, ibid.) [documented] — relevant to the chapel's future status.

**The statue:**
- Location: **near/next to the entrance** of the church — Times-Picayune photos (Chris Granger, Aug 2017) caption it "next to the entrance"; the cross in its hand reads **HODIE** (nola.com 300-for-300, ibid.) [documented]. Practitioner lore treats this door-side placement as meaningful (see §3 altar rules).
- Iconography: young Roman soldier/centurion, helmetless and swordless, **palm branch** (martyrdom) in one hand, raised **cross marked HODIE** ("today") in the other; canonical iconography adds a **crow crushed underfoot** emitting a ribbon reading **CRAS** ("tomorrow") (nola.com ibid.; https://www.luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html) [documented iconography].
- **The crate legend, New Orleans version:** around **1921** (per the Times-Picayune's telling), nuns at Our Lady of Guadalupe received a crate marked "expedite" — possibly a shipping instruction for a rush order — containing an unidentified Roman-soldier statue; they declared him St. Expedite and installed him near the entrance (nola.com ibid.) [legend — the paper itself files the story as "leaning toward the legendary"].
  - Variant tellings: two crates from Spain during/after the Spanish colonial era (1763–1803), one with the commissioned Virgin Mary statue, the second labeled only EXPEDITE (https://www.luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html — which calls it "humourously apocryphal"; also https://neworleansghosttour.com/our-lady-of-guadalupe/) [legend].
  - Variant with the crate arriving at **St. Louis Cathedral** after the Louisiana Purchase and being opened by **Creole-speaking Haitian workmen** who named the saint (http://hoodooq.blogspot.com/2010/10/samedi-saint-expedite.html) [legend].
  - Debunking: near-identical stories exist in **France and Sicily** (one places nuns opening an "expedite" crate of a saint's relics in 18th-century Paris); anthropologists treat the New Orleans version as a migrated urban legend (nola.com ibid.; https://jamesduvalier.com/saint-expedite-origins/) [documented-as-legend].
- **The "real" saint:** Roman martyrology lists an Expeditus, 4th-c. Armenian-garrison centurion, feast **April 19** (some sources add April 22); legend of the demon crow croaking "cras" (tomorrow), crushed with the cry "Hodie!" (today); martyred under Diocletian, dated in devotional tellings to April 19, 301 (https://jamesduvalier.com/saint-expedite-origins/; luckymojo.com ibid.) [legend/hagiography — the saint's historicity is itself doubted; never a Vatican-promoted cultus].
- Lucky Mojo adds the *pro-CRAS-tination* pun (crows croak "cras cras"), claims medieval priests used the image to warn against postponing conversion, and even links the tradition to Poe's raven (luckymojo.com ibid.) [practitioner-consensus for the pun; the Poe link is the site's own speculation].
- Who visits: "Catholics, as well as some local followers of Voodoo" (Wikipedia OLG church, ibid.) [documented]; the church sits **across Rampart from St. Louis Cemetery No. 1** — both are described as Voodoo pilgrimage destinations, and proximity to Marie Laveau's tomb feeds the overlap (https://jamesduvalier.com/saint-expedite-origins/) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Marie Laveau tomb link:** at St. Louis Cemetery No. 1, "offerings of pound cake are left to the statue of Saint Expedite; these offerings are believed to expedite the favors asked of the Voodoo queen. Saint Expedite represents the spirit standing between life and death." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Voodoo) [documented as reported practice; note this is a *Voodoo*-register usage].
- NOLA saint-culture context: St. Expedite ranks with St. Jude (lost causes), St. Anne (husband-finding), St. Joseph (upside-down statue to sell a house), St. Anthony ("Saint Ant'ny," lost things) as a beloved local saint; the nola.com piece emphasizes he "pulls together different threads" — Catholic credibility, punning backstory, and adoption by Voodoo practitioners (nola.com ibid.) [documented].

## 2. Offerings

Blur note: this is the section where **hoodoo rootwork and folk-Catholic devotion are fully merged** in sources; the same offering list is given by Catholic-leaning devotees and rootworkers alike.

- **Pound cake** — THE signature offering, given **after** the favor (see §3 contract). "Saint Expedite's absolute favorite offering is pound cake, and every petition fulfilled by him must be met with at least one slice" (http://saint-expedite.blogspot.com/2014/12/st-expedites-magickal-pound-cake.html) [practitioner-consensus].
  - **Why Sara Lee:** New Orleans tradition specifically names **Sara Lee brand** — "most likely because it is cheap, easy to get, and for a few bucks you can keep a loaf in the freezer ready to pull out and slice off a piece at a moment's notice" (saint-expedite.blogspot.com ibid.; same claim at https://jamesduvalier.com/pound-cake-for-saint-expedite/) [practitioner-consensus]. Lucky Mojo attributes the Sara Lee specification to "one New Orleans informant," homemade serving equally well (luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html) [practitioner-consensus]. Sphere + Sundry repeats "Sarah Lee brand… rumored favorite" (https://sphereandsundry.com/working-with-st-expedite-altar-offerings-and-ritual-for-prayers-petitions/) [practitioner-consensus].
  - **Ingredient symbolism** (one practitioner's gloss): butter = wealth/prosperity, sugar = sweetening, lemon extract = cutting obstacles, eggs & flour = abundance; a 1920s *New Royal Cook Book* recipe circulates with magical annotations; variations include cake balls, bundt, layered, swirled (saint-expedite.blogspot.com pound-cake post) [practitioner-consensus, single-source gloss].
  - Served sliced; left on the altar **1–2 days** then "disposed of respectfully — never consumed by the petitioner" (https://www.aromags.com/how-to-use-saint-expedite-in-petition-work/) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Glass of water** — minimum standing offering: "at minimum put out a clean glass of cool water for him before starting" (search-consensus incl. https://www.kitchentoad.com/blog/saint-expedite-the-saint-of-urgent-matters, https://www.bearryver.com/working-with-saint-expedite/) [practitioner-consensus]. After a 7-day candle burns out, some fill the empty candle glass with water; Lucky Mojo instead re-uses the spent glass as a **flower vase** (jamesduvalier.com/saint-expedite-origins/; luckymojo.com ibid.) [practitioner-consensus, variant].
- **Red flowers** — especially roses; bouquet given on completion, often placed in the spent candle glass (jamesduvalier.com ibid.; luckymojo.com ibid.) [practitioner-consensus]. The **Hyatt corpus** (Harry Middleton Hyatt fieldwork, 1935–39) records a New Orleans informant: "He takes unless yo' give him flowers. Yo' must give him flowers because if not, then someone out of the house will pass on." (quoted at luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html) [documented — earliest field-recorded attestation found in this crawl; note flowers, not cake, are the recorded debt].
- **Dimes/coins** — "he and his team like… dimes, keys, candy, and toys"; one formula uses **7 silver dimes** (pre-1965 silver dimes carry potency/protection significance in hoodoo generally) (kitchentoad.com ibid.; https://revolutionarymystic.com/blogs/spellwork-astrology-wolfdogs/an-essential-guide-to-working-with-saint-expedite-in-your-spiritual-practice) [practitioner-consensus]. Coins/paper money/mock money/checks also listed (sphereandsundry.com ibid.) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Red candles** — his color; 7-day glass-encased vigil lights, classically lit on **Wednesday** (jamesduvalier.com ibid.; luckymojo.com ibid.) [practitioner-consensus]. Variants: yellow for communication/job work, white for blessing/breakthrough (aromags.com ibid.) [commercial-claim/practitioner method].
- Extended offering lists: coffee, rum, red wine, palm fronds, holy water, rosaries, **skeleton keys and lock picks**, crow feathers, red/green/gold items, candy, toys (aromags.com; sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus, long tail].
- Disposal: leave until wilting/spoilage, water plants with the offering water, compost or leave for wildlife (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus, modern gloss].
- Numbers: 3, 7, 9, 21 recur; he "favors triplicities" (aromags.com; sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus].

## 3. Petition structure — the ask / promise / pay contract

- **Core contract:** petition with a promised payment → favor delivered fast → deliver the offering AND public thanks. Lucky Mojo: "you must never seek the aid of Expedite unless you are prepared to give this tribute after the work is done, **or he will take back all the good he did for you, and more besides**" (luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html) [practitioner-consensus — the single most repeated rule in the crawl]. aromaG's: "failing to deliver a promised offering is considered disrespectful and may end your working relationship" (aromags.com ibid.) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Offering is withheld until delivery** — pay on results, not up front: pound cake and coffee go on the altar *after* results arrive (aromags.com) [practitioner-consensus]. Sphere + Sundry documents a haggling variant: "pay half upfront and half upon fulfillment to incentivize faster results" (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus, variant]. The standing glass of water is the exception — maintained throughout.
- **Specificity rule:** petitions must state exact amount, reason, deadline — "$200 for utilities by Thursday," not vague appeals; name involved parties, location, and demand speed ("today!", "now!", "without delay!") even for distant deadlines (aromags.com; sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Sample working (fast money, aromaG's):** cleanse red 7-day candle with Florida Water → write petition, fold **toward** you, place under candle → dress with St. Expedite oil + cinnamon → image behind candle → light, speak the request aloud once → burn until granted (aromags.com) [practitioner method]. Court-case, job, and road-opener variants use documents/resumes under or beside the candle; Road Opener oil + cayenne for stuck situations (aromags.com; sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner method].
- **Prayer forms:** call his name three times ("Saint Expedite, Saint Expedite, Saint Expedite!"), knock three times or ring a bell, invoke the crow story, close "HODIE, HODIE, HODIE! Amen" (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner method]. Lucky Mojo prints four prayers, one commanding: "Expedite now what I ask of you, this very second. Don't waste another day" — recited daily until results (luckymojo.com) [practitioner method]. A widely shared Catholic-register novena prayer runs "Lord, our God and Father, you allow us to invoke St. Expedite as intercessor, especially in cases that we consider urgent…" with the sign of the cross (http://saint-expedite.blogspot.com/p/payers-and-petitions-to-st-expedite.html) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Altar placement rules:**
  - **Doorways:** "Saint Expedite prefers doorways… set up your altar near the front or back door," the more primary the entrance the better, indoors or out — he is "hot, way-opening" energy (sphereandsundry.com; echoed at nubianimpulse.com, bearryver.com) [practitioner-consensus]. Resonates with the church statue's next-to-the-entrance position (§1) — practitioners cite that placement as precedent [practitioner-consensus inference].
  - **Not in bedrooms** — his active energy disturbs sleep (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus, modern].
  - **Triangle layout:** water glass front-left, candle middle-rear (apex), statue/image front-right; with glass-encased vigils, water simply to the candle's left (luckymojo.com; saint-expedite.blogspot.com altar post) [practitioner-consensus].
  - Red or white altar cloth; directional preference south/east in one modern feng-shui-inflected source (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner, single-source].
- **Timing:** Wednesday is his day (Mercury's day); planetary-hours refinements exist in astrologically inclined practice; feast April 19 (or 22), though "many believe he comes whenever called" (jamesduvalier.com; sphereandsundry.com; aromags.com) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Coercive variants:** flipping his icon **upside-down** until the favor lands so he'll hurry to be righted (sphereandsundry.com, whose author "feels rude" doing it) [practitioner, attested variant — parallels upside-down St. Joseph and St. Anthony customs].
- **Speed expectation:** movement immediately to within days; testimonial language runs "within hours," "within 15 hours," "within a week" (saint-expedite.blogspot.com gratitude page; aromags.com) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Cautions circulating:** don't invoke during surgery (the surgeon might rush); healing requests max once weekly, his heat "can tax the body"; magic must be backed by mundane action (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner, modern gloss].
- Domains: urgent matters generally; money/finance especially; also employment, court cases, housing/real-estate (altar near the front door, prayer cards under welcome mats), gambling luck, immigration, reconciliations, ending procrastination (jamesduvalier.com; sphereandsundry.com; saint-expedite.blogspot.com testimonials) [practitioner-consensus].

## 4. Newspaper thanks & public gratitude

- **The rule:** thanks must be public. "You should also thank him publicly to let others know of his generosity. If you do not thank him in this manner, he will take back your request and then some" (search-consensus, e.g. https://mikkimcoy.wordpress.com/2010/11/29/saint-expedite/) [practitioner-consensus].
- **Newspaper form:** placing a classified/personal-column ad "thanking Saint Expedite, so that his name and fame will grow"; his name "is regularly mentioned with gratitude in the personal column of the local paper, alongside St Jude" — the custom is borrowed from/shared with the older St. Jude thank-you-notice tradition (search results incl. mikkimcoy.wordpress.com; molossia.org/stexpeditus.html) [practitioner-consensus; TODO: the Louisiana Folklife essay and Times-Picayune classified archives are the [documented] anchors — site was down at crawl time; Tulane's Times-Picayune libguide (https://libguides.tulane.edu/news_newspapers/times_picayune) is the archive route].
- Lucky Mojo frames the newspaper-publication custom as evidence of his **trickster-spirit character**: publishing gratitude reflects "Expedite's analogy to African trickster spirits rather than mainstream Catholic saint intercession" (luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html) [practitioner-analysis].
- **Digital continuation:** the tradition has visibly migrated online. The Conjurer's Guide blog runs a standing "Public Declarations of Gratitude to St. Expedite" page with hundreds of testimonials from 2016 onward (money, jobs, housing, court, immigration, health, reconciliation), users self-describing as his "faithful servants" and "friends" (http://saint-expedite.blogspot.com/p/payers-and-petitions-to-st-expedite.html) [documented — the page itself is the artifact]. A dedicated tributes site solicits the same (https://www.saintexpedite.com/tributes/) [documented artifact / commercial adjacency]. Sphere + Sundry explicitly modernizes: "Expedite loooves to be publicly recognized" — social-media posts, hashtags, comment-section testimony function as the payment (sphereandsundry.com) [practitioner-consensus].
- One testimonial records tithing 10% of received funds and "giving to the poor in your honor" as alternate public payments; thanksgiving Masses also appear (saint-expedite.blogspot.com gratitude page) [documented testimonials].

## 5. Syncretism & spirit associations

Blur note: this is where the *Voodoo* (religion) register dominates; hoodoo sources borrow the associations loosely, and Catholic devotees ignore them entirely. All three registers use the same statue.

- **Baron Samedi (lord of the Ghede, dead/cemetery lwa):** "In New Orleans Voudou St. Expedite is syncretized with the lwa Baron Samedi" (The Conjurer's Guide, via https://www.facebook.com/saintexpedito/posts/1137677592941573/) [practitioner-consensus for New Orleans]. HoodooQ details the reasoning and the key geographic limit: the association is **New Orleans-specific and NOT general in Haiti** — "In Haitian Voudon the Ghede tend not to be represented by Saints. A cross will do nicely, particularly if it is painted black or purple" (http://hoodooq.blogspot.com/2010/10/samedi-saint-expedite.html) [practitioner-consensus, well-argued]. Fit factors: statue faces St. Louis Cemetery No. 1; Wikipedia's "spirit standing between life and death" phrasing at Laveau's tomb (§1) matches the Ghede's threshold role [practitioner-analysis].
- **Mercury / messenger-trickster complex:** Lucky Mojo syncretizes him with **Mercury** (quicksilver as his metal, Wednesday as his day) and associates him with "**Elegua, Legba, Baron Samedi, Bonsu**, and so forth… the messengers and tricksters in the Lukumi/Santeria, Voodoo/Vodoun, and Obeah pantheons" (luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html) [practitioner-consensus in the rootwork/botánica world; note this is an *associative* list, not a claim of formal camino correspondence]. The doorway altar rule (§3) is Elegua/Legba-shaped [practitioner-analysis].
- **Dissent:** James Duvalier (New Orleans-lineage practitioner-author) explicitly resists over-syncretizing: "Saint Expedite is not a typical Voodoo spirit and he is not of African origin, so his service is a bit different and less complicated" — no Baron Samedi mention in his origins piece (https://jamesduvalier.com/saint-expedite-origins/) [practitioner, notable dissent].
- **General mechanism** (for wiki framing): lwa-saint correspondence arose from practicing African religion "openly under the nose of Catholic slaveholders" — every lwa acquired a chromolithograph/statue counterpart (hoodooq.blogspot.com ibid.; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Voodoo: Papa Lébat↔St. Peter, Mama You↔Virgin Mary, St. Anthony's popularity tied to Bakongo patronage) [documented]. 1930s–40s interviews suggest late-19th-c. Voodoo "primarily entailed supplications to the saints for assistance" (Wikipedia ibid.) [documented] — i.e., saint-petitioning was already the religion's everyday texture, into which Expedite slots natively.
- Historical hoodoo attestation: the Hyatt 1935–39 New Orleans informant quote (§2) is the crawl's earliest documented Expedite-in-hoodoo record [documented].

## Cross-cutting notes for the wiki article

- Three registers, one statue: folk-Catholic devotion (novena prayers, Masses of thanksgiving), hoodoo rootwork (candle-dressing, petition papers, oils), New Orleans Voodoo (Baron Samedi/Ghede syncretism, Laveau-tomb offerings). Sources rarely stay in one register; the practice's identity IS the overlap.
- The reciprocity contract (pay-on-delivery + public advertisement + take-back sanction) is the structural signature distinguishing Expedite work from mainstream saint intercession — analytically parallel to contested/transactional spirit bargains elsewhere (cf. trickster-payment framing at luckymojo.com).
- Earliest hard attestation found: Hyatt informant, New Orleans, 1935–39 (flowers-or-death debt). The 1921 crate date (Times-Picayune) and the 1918 Oblate takeover bracket the statue's plausible installation window; the Spanish-era version is the older, borrowed legend.
- Re-crawl when available: louisianafolklife.org essay (scholarly anchor, likely has Times-Picayune notice history and South Louisiana (non-NOLA) Catholic usage); readersandrootworkers.org wiki page (via browser).
