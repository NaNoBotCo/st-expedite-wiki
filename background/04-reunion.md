## 1. The saint himself (background to the island cult)

- Traditional hagiography: Roman officer, commander in the XII legion ("Fulminata"), stationed at Melitene (Roman Armenia); martyred (flogged, then **beheaded**) under Diocletian, conventionally 303 CE. [legend] (7L1; VG: "flagellé jusqu'au sang et décapité"; ORAT)
- Historicity is rejected by critical scholarship: Bollandist H. Delehaye (*Analecta Bollandiana* 1899, 1906) showed an "Expeditus" appears at 19 April in the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* among six Melitene martyrs, but held that a name in a martyrology proves neither existence nor ancient cult; possibly a scribal corruption of "Elpidius" from the 412 Syriac martyrology. No relics, no continuity of cult before the 19th c. [documented] (GAUTHIER)
- The famous alternative origin — a relic crate from Rome labelled *spedito / espedito / "in expedito"* ("dispatched"), misread by nuns (Paris, or German nuns in Christian Morgenstern's satirical 1910 poem "Sankt-Expeditus") as the saint's name. [legend] (7L1; ORAT; MART; SEFR)
- Iconography: Roman soldier; raised cross inscribed **"hodie"** (today); martyr's palm; at his feet a raven (crow) bearing a phylactery **"cras"** (tomorrow) — the devil's raven croaks "cras! cras!" (procrastinate), the saint answers "hodie!". Wears the *paludamentum*, the general's cloak — rendered bright red in Réunion. [documented (iconographic convention)] (7L1; ORAT; DPR)
- Parish reading of the emblem (Notre-Dame de la Délivrance): the raven is "l'ange des ténèbres" (the angel of darkness) urging delay; "Demain, c'est peut-être jamais !" (Tomorrow is perhaps never!). [practitioner-consensus] (7L1)
- Vatican action: the saint was struck from the martyrology and his images ordered removed from churches in 1905/1906 — sources disagree on which pope (Pius X per GAUTHIER; Pius XI per 7L1, ORAT, VG; art historian Louis Réau is 7L1's cited authority) and the measure is described as "allegedly" and as completely ineffective. He was never canonized, but also never formally condemned, and still figures in the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum*. [scholarly-contested — date/pope; documented — never canonized, never condemned] (GAUTHIER; 7L1; ORAT; VG)

## 2. History & spread on Réunion

- **Origin story:** a Réunionnaise stranded in France at the end of WWI — 7L1 names her **Fanny Fleurié**, stuck in Marseille during the 1918 Spanish-flu shipping closures; SEFR/MART call her **Mme Chatel**, "a wealthy Saint-Denis woman" — prayed before the Saint-Expédit statue in the church of **Saint-Cannat les Prêcheurs, Marseille**, vowing devotion if she got passage home. Within (three) days she had her ticket; back home she donated a statue to **Notre-Dame de la Délivrance** (quartier Petite-Île, Saint-Denis). [legend — the narrative; documented — the statue itself] (7L1; SEFR; MART; GAUTHIER)
- **First statue blessed 3 May 1931** at Notre-Dame de la Délivrance: "La statue y est installée en bonne place, sur un piédestal, entourée d'une grille. Elle a été bénie le 3 mai 1931." (The statue was installed in a place of honour, on a pedestal, surrounded by a railing. It was blessed on 3 May 1931.) Bishop **Mgr Cléret de Langavant** identified this as the cult's official Réunion starting point. Note the episcopal blessing = initial institutional legitimacy. [documented] (7L1; SEFR)
- **Dating dispute:** Prosper Eve puts the cult's emergence in the 1920s; Christian Barat argues a pre-1920s presence; Reignier's 2001 thesis presents conflicting evidence. INDE says the saint "arrived in the 1920s", with "Réunionization" phases 1935–1970. [scholarly-contested] (7L1; INDE)
- Earliest private chapels: **Bras-Fusil, Saint-Benoît (1938)** and **Baril, Saint-Philippe (1939)**; also Rivière-des-Pluies and Saint-Gilles-les-Bains (undated, per Daniel Vaxélaire 1996). [documented] (7L1)
- **Joseph Tafiki (1905–2002)**, "le serviteur des pauvres" (servant of the poor), farmer-peddler of Saint-Philippe, father of 14, member of the Conférence de Saint-Vincent-de-Paul: built in **1933** at **Mare Longue** (RN2, between Saint-Joseph and Saint-Philippe) what grew from a simple niche into a three-building chapel complex (main chapel red and blue), holding an exceptional concentration of Saint-Expédit images and ex-votos; still an active pilgrimage point. [documented] (7L4)
- 1947: Prefect **Paul Demange** (WWII Resistance figure, Neuengamme deportee, already a devotee in France) erected an oratory on the ramparts of La Montagne road; destroyed repeatedly by an "orthodox objector", it finally disappeared in the early 2000s. [documented] (7L1)
- Growth context: the saint took root amid interwar poverty, alcoholism and disease; he became known for unexpected healings, conflict resolution — and harm. Reignier attributes the departure from Christian charity ideals to "the Catholic church's difficulty reproducing the European model among transplanted, decultured populations". [documented — Reignier's argument] (INDE)
- GAUTHIER stresses this is **not a residual/declining cult** and suggests post-Vatican-II reforms (removal of familiar intercessors) may have accelerated adoption. [documented — her argument] (GAUTHIER)
- Wider créole/Indian-Ocean distribution: Guadeloupe (tops saint-popularity rankings), Haiti (invoked with reversed images for hexing; independent since 1804, which undercuts a purely colonial-transmission model), Brazil (explosive growth after a printer's 1997 vow; 200,000 at São Paulo 19-April ceremonies by 2004), Mauritius (~15 altars at Pamplemousses Hospital entrance mixing Shiva/Parvati altars with Saint-Expédit, who draws the greatest devotion). GAUTHIER argues the distribution "à des milliers de kilomètres de distance" (thousands of kilometres apart) points to diffusion among populations of African origin during slavery's "sombres heures" (dark hours) who recognized "l'un des leurs" (one of their own). [documented — reported facts; scholarly-contested — the diffusion thesis] (GAUTHIER; 7L3)
- Madagascar feedback loop: "Saint-Expédit malgache" statuettes are carved in Madagascar and sold at the Grand Marché, Saint-Denis — manufactured **for the Réunionnais market**; the cult is unknown in Madagascar itself. [documented] (7L3)

## 3. Census numbers

- **Prosper Eve, 1977** (first census, by population survey; *La religion populaire à La Réunion*, Institut de linguistique et d'anthropologie, Université de la Réunion, 1985): ≈100 edifices — **92 oratories + 8 chapels**. [documented] (7L1, citing Reignier thesis p. 410; DPR; search-confirmed at https://7lameslamer.net/au-nom-de-saint-expedit/)
- **Philippe Reignier, 1998 census: 338 oratories + 31 chapels across 250 sites** — a near-quadrupling in twenty years, "une véritable «explosion» du phénomène" (a veritable "explosion" of the phenomenon). [documented] (7L1; DPR)
- GAUTHIER gives slightly different figures: **94 oratories in 1970; 337 in 1997** (she cites Reignier's 1997 exhibition catalogue *P'ti Bon Dieu. Le culte de saint Expédit*, Musée Stella Matutina). The 1970/94 vs 1977/92 discrepancy between Gauthier and 7L1/DPR is unresolved; 7L1 cites Reignier's thesis directly (p. 410) and is probably the better reading. [scholarly-contested — exact baseline year/count] (GAUTHIER; 7L1)
- Density: about **one shrine per 2,000 inhabitants** by the late 20th c. [documented] (7L1)
- Later figures: "over 340" (ORAT), "over 400 altars/oratories/chapels" (7L3), "~350 chapels" (OUEST) — no formal census after 1998 found. [practitioner-consensus] (ORAT; 7L3; OUEST)
- DPR notes population growth (500k → 700k) explains part but not all of the expansion; syncretic drivers did the rest. [documented — the argument] (DPR)
- **Reignier's thesis:** *Saint Expédit à la Réunion : monographie*, doctorat d'anthropologie, Université de La Réunion, 2001, dir. **Bernard Champion**, 450 pp. Approach: not a mythography but a mapping of "the transformation sites where his iconostasis operates" — objects + images + ethnographic encounters treated as one integrated mythic-ritual system, a "portrait" of a figure defined primarily by a name, read as the mental universe of 20th-c. Réunionnais. Opening frame: "for about seventy years, a visible and mysterious figure has stood uncovered in the landscapes and occupied the thoughts of part of the Réunionese people." [documented] (THESE; 7L1)

## 4. Shrine form & siting

- Form: small roadside oratories, typically **~1 m in each dimension**, of concrete or sheet metal, sometimes nestled into rock outcrops; statues of a few decimetres. Uniformly painted **rouge vif / rouge sang** (bright/blood red). [documented] (GAUTHIER; ORAT; DPR)
- Contents/offerings: candles (proportionally more than in churches), incense, coins, fresh and plastic flowers, red cloth/fabric bindings, ex-votos and thank-you plaques, plus mainstream Catholic statuary (Notre-Dame de Lourdes, Sacré-Cœur, Virgin Mary) sharing the niche. [documented] (GAUTHIER; ORAT; VG; MART)
- Siting: roadsides, **carrefours (crossroads)**, ravines, radiers (fords/causeways) and dangerous curves, roundabouts, "au détour des ravines, au fond d'un cirque et même sur le volcan" (round ravine bends, deep in a cirque, even on the volcano); "spécialement dans les Hauts" (especially in the Highlands); increasingly on private property for family/property protection — but **never inside homes, considered dangerous**. [documented] (GAUTHIER; 7L1; ORAT; VG; WIKI-EM; LINFO-snippet)
- **Why those places — the wandering-souls explanation (Prosper Eve):** "On trouve un oratoire dédié à Saint Expédit là où il y a eu un accident. Généralement dans les virages un peu dangereux, on a l'habitude de considérer que l'âme erre. Pour apaiser l'âme, la vertu de Saint Expédit c'est de l'apaiser pour que cette âme n'ait aucune influence sur les vivants qui circulent dans cette zone-là." (You find an oratory dedicated to Saint Expédit where there has been an accident. Generally in somewhat dangerous bends, the soul is customarily held to wander. Saint Expédit's virtue is to appease that soul so it has no influence on the living who pass through that zone.) Hence the tourism-office framing of the shrines as a "sépulture religieuse sur les routes" (religious burial/grave on the roads). [documented — Eve's analysis; practitioner-consensus — the belief] (LINFO via search snippet; OUEST)
- Visibility is intentional: the red *paludamentum*-derived paint marks roadsides, roundabouts and river fords so shrines are seen. [documented] (7L1)
- Church-interior installations with the original statues persist at Notre-Dame de la Délivrance and Saint-Leu; chapels at Notre-Dame des Champs (Étang Saint-Leu — statue vanished), Notre-Dame de Pellevoisin (Rivière-du-Mât-les-Bas — statue moved out to an attached oratory), Notre-Dame de l'Escalier (Salazie). An oratory at CHD Bellepierre hospital (Saint-Denis) was demolished in renovations. [documented] (7L1; DPR)
- Shrines self-propagate: friends built a joke birthday oratory for one Christophe; anonymous devotees lit candles overnight and it is now a maintained working shrine. [documented — reported anecdote] (7L2)

## 5. Practices — bright and dark

**Bright side.** Petitions: healing, employment, money/loans, exam success (the Délivrance parish's imprimatur booklet includes prayers for children's schooling, driving-test success, suicide prevention, and rapid resolution of court cases). He is the saint of urgent and desperate causes — "Ti Bondieu" (little god), "le Saint patron des gens ordinaires et plus généralement déshérités" (patron of ordinary and, more broadly, dispossessed people). [documented] (GAUTHIER; 7L1; 7L2; VG; SEFR)

**The bargain.** The relationship is transactional, not devotional-emulative — GAUTHIER: "c'est donnant donnant" (strictly quid pro quo); the cult privileges "l'observation scrupuleuse de rites" (scrupulous observance of rites) over prayer. A vow ("promès" in créole) binds: once granted, **you must pay, and fast** — "Il est recommandé de tenir parole dans les délais les plus brefs" (keep your word in the shortest possible time), for "les parjures furent durement punis" (oath-breakers were harshly punished); the threat of losing the granted grace — or worse — hangs over beneficiaries. [documented — described across sources; practitioner-consensus — the sanction] (GAUTHIER; VG; MART)
- Concrete vow-forms: red flowers and candles; **seven consecutive Fridays** of candle-lighting with specific foods; wedding veils/bridal dresses offered to "rendre les promesses" (fulfil the promises); women observing "carême" — meatless diet and sexual abstinence — while soliciting a grace (a borrowing from Hindu practice). [documented] (7L2)
- Literary echo: Daniel Honoré's novel *Vativien* — Antonia's seven-Friday vow for her husband's recovery, granted "but at what cost?". [documented — the novel] (7L2)

**Dark side.** Openly ambivalent power:
- Invoked to eliminate business or romantic rivals, for vengeance, and for curses; sorcerers, "malbars" (here = ritual specialists), and diviners employ him to "envouter" (hex) victims — rendering them "docile", "couillon" (fooled/stupid), "affaibli" (weakened). [documented — reported practice] (GAUTHIER; 7L2; SEFR; ORAT)
- A resident's warning: some people **place photographs at the saint's feet asking for the pictured person's death**. [practitioner-consensus] (linfo.re, "Saint Expédit : le protecteur des réunionnais mais pas que…", via search — https://www.linfo.re/la-reunion/societe/saint-expedit-ou-le-protecteur-des-reunionnais)
- GAUTHIER quotes a participant: "les gens de l'île ne vous diront pas exactement le fin mot de l'histoire, car si ça vous prenait l'envie de tester et de prier pour qu'ils perdent un être cher par exemple ?" (island people won't tell you the whole story — what if you took it into your head to test it and pray for them to lose a loved one, for example?). [documented — quoted testimony] (GAUTHIER)
- **Decapitated statues — verified, with multiple readings.** GAUTHIER (from Reignier's statistics): **28% of statues are decapitated**, "ritual mutilation reflecting aggressive petitions". Competing explanations across sources: (1) revenge by devotees whose petitions failed — "les gens qui ont été déçus par le Saint se vengent en faisant cela pour montrer leur mécontentement" (people disappointed by the Saint take revenge this way to show their displeasure); (2) cutting/breaking an existing curse; (3) commemoration of the saint's own beheading; (4) mutilation performed in the course of sorcery rituals. So yes — beheading-when-vows-fail is one documented reading, but only one of several. [documented — the phenomenon and %; scholarly-contested — the meaning] (GAUTHIER; VG; SEFR; MART)
- **Pin-stuck figures: NOT confirmed for Réunion.** Targeted searching found no Réunion-specific attestation of needles/pins/effigy-binding at Expédit shrines (that motif is attested for Haiti/vodou contexts, and SEFR links him to Haitian black-magic use). Treat as unverified for Réunion pending Reignier's thesis itself. [scholarly-contested / unverified]
- Statues are treated **as living bodies**: "Il ne faut pas jeter les vieilles statues abîmées, c'est comme un corps. C'est comme un respect. Quand on enlève… il faut faire un grand travail, une cérémonie compliquée avec un prêtre" (You must not throw away old damaged statues — it's like a body. It's a matter of respect. Removing one requires a big operation, a complicated ceremony with a priest). Damaged statues stay in place and keep receiving offerings. [documented — testimony] (7L2; VG)
- Character, per synthesized testimony (7L2): positive — "bon, sérieux, franc, droit, rapide… gardien… guerrier… Il éclaire le chemin. Il est pour la vérité" (good, serious, frank, upright, fast… guardian… warrior… He lights the way. He stands for truth); negative — "méchant, jaloux, rancunier… Il ne plaisante pas… Il ne pardonne pas… Il est amoureux. Il 'gâche' les ménages" (mean, jealous, grudge-holding… He doesn't joke… He doesn't forgive… He is amorous. He 'wrecks' households). Gendered lore: women obtain graces more easily; he is "amoureux et jaloux" (in love and jealous) — one legend has him jilted by a fiancée; another tradition has him helping men marry via offered wedding garments. [practitioner-consensus] (7L2)
- Secrecy: the cult is socially devalued, so shrine visits happen discreetly — "ce culte étant socialement dévalorisé la fréquentation de ces oratoires se fait en secret"; adherent numbers unknown. Tourist guides steer visitors away, associating oratories with "forces occultes". [documented] (VG; ORAT)
- The speed theme: he is "vif" (quick), resolving cases rapidly and efficiently — and GAUTHIER (via Monique Augras on Brazil) reads the late-20th-c. valorization of expeditiousness as of a piece with consumer society's implicit motto "tout avoir, tout de suite" (have everything, right now): "la dimension magique du recours aux saints… paraît s'inscrire dans le droit fil de la société de consommation…". [documented — the analysis] (GAUTHIER)

## 6. Syncretism

- Historical substrate: after abolition (1848), Tamil-origin indentured labourers ("**Malbars**") replaced enslaved African workers on the plantations; their descendants practice a Catholicism interwoven with popular Hinduism. [documented] (GAUTHIER)
- **Kali / "Karly" / Carli:** the blood-red of the shrines is widely linked to Kali's iconography (standing on corpses, garland of skulls, unlimited destructive power); Saint-Expédit is directly called "Karly" by some devotees. Reignier: "Protecteur et vengeur, on l'associe à la déesse Kali… capable de tout prendre comme de tout donner" (Protector and avenger, he is associated with the goddess Kali… capable of taking everything as of giving everything). [documented] (GAUTHIER; 7L2; INDE)
- **Mardévirin / Mardé Vira:** Tamil hero-guardian ("gardien du temps", impassive warrior, justice-bringer, protector), associated with Kali; Expédit assimilated to him (ORAT spells the pair "Karli ou Mardé-Vira", glossed as wealth and strength). **Suprâmanyel** (Murugan), demon-slayer with the lance, appears as a third analogue (compared to Saint Michael). Shiva is referenced without elaboration. [documented] (7L2; INDE; ORAT)
- Layered red-explanations collected from devotees (7L2): "C'est peint en rouge car il ressemble bien au malbar, à la religion hindoue" (painted red because he's really like the Malbar, the Hindu religion); "c'est la couleur du sang" (it's the colour of blood); "c'est un soldat romain qui a été crucifié comme Jésus" (he's a Roman soldier crucified like Jesus). Official/etymological reading: the red is the *paludamentum*. He is summarized as "un malbar-catholique". For Malbars red = blood, life and death — a sacred colour. [practitioner-consensus, multiple codings] (7L2; 7L1; ORAT via search)
- Borrowed Hindu rites acknowledged by devotees: meat abstinence (carême) and **prayers in Tamil**. [documented] (GAUTHIER)
- Malagasy and Muslim engagement: Malagasy-descended Réunionnais fold him into ancestral veneration; Muslims too (per ORAT); Malagasy "grafts" are credited with part of the cult's expansion; the standard formula is a syncretism "mêlant l'héritage catholique aux croyances venues de Madagascar ou d'Inde" (blending the Catholic heritage with beliefs from Madagascar or India). [documented — broad pattern; thin on specifics] (ORAT; 7L1; WIKI-EM citing Gauthier)
- The Malabar association **feeds the fear**: GAUTHIER notes the syncretism intensifies créole suspicion of the "negative potential power" of Malabar cults, heightening the saint's ambiguity. [documented] (GAUTHIER)
- Important scholarly nuance (Reignier, via INDE): the Hindu associations proceed by **comparison and cumulation, not fusion** — "no confusion of personalities or attributes occurs". The word "syncretism" is thus itself contested for this case. INDE argues it was Hinduism as a persecuted counter-culture that made the unorthodox Christian figure into the recognized divine mediator. [scholarly-contested — the mechanism] (INDE)
- The cult crosses communities: used by Catholics, Hindus, Malagasy-ancestry practitioners, and occult specialists alike. [documented] (ORAT)

## 7. Church relations

- Sequence: initial legitimacy (episcopal blessing 1931, statue inside a church) → Rome's 1905/06 removal from the martyrology (ignored) → **1965: removal from the Réunion diocesan calendar**, formalizing disapproval → present-day ambivalence. [documented; the 1905/06 details scholarly-contested] (7L1; SEFR; ORAT)
- ORAT: "Priests rarely tolerate this devotion in parishes"; VG: "Si certains prêtres le tolèrent, la plupart déconseillent ou même interdisent ce culte" (some priests tolerate it, most advise against or even forbid it), citing magical connotations and the saint's unverified existence. [documented — reported clerical attitudes] (ORAT; VG)
- Yet the Church **never condemned** the cult, and an annual 19-April Mass is celebrated at Notre-Dame de la Délivrance. [documented] (7L1; ORAT via search)
- **Bishop Gilbert Aubry** (bishop of Saint-Denis 1976–2023) characterized it as "un «culte carrefour» où viennent se rencontrer diverses traditions ethnoculturelles et religieuses" (a "crossroads cult" where diverse ethnocultural and religious traditions come to meet), a current transmitting "both the best and the worst" within Christian iconography, foregrounding the martyred Roman soldier faithful to his beliefs. No formal pastoral letter on the cult was located; this characterization (via 7L3/DPR) is the closest thing to an official diocesan position found. [documented — the quote; no formal pastoral document found] (7L3; DPR)
- **Père Lilian Payet**, curé of Notre-Dame de la Délivrance, published an imprimatur-approved booklet (red cover, the traditional colour) of 16 prayers plus hagiography — a channeling strategy: "If one judges by increasing candles and visitors, Saint-Expédit's attendance hasn't diminished… I remind people that Saint-Expédit loved Christ and died a martyr" ("quelqu'un qui a aimé le Christ, et qui est mort en martyre"). The parish also distributes a 24-prayer booklet. [documented] (7L1; 7L3; DPR; 7L2)
- GAUTHIER's framing: the Réunionnais "privatized" and "creolized" the saint against institutional reservations — a "contre-pouvoir" (counter-power), a "Ti bondieu" more accessible and effective than the official Church; ORAT similarly: honoured mainly outside official churches, "almost in contestation of the Catholic hierarchy". [documented — analyses] (GAUTHIER; ORAT via search)
