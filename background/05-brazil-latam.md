# Santo Expedito / San Expedito in Brazil, Spanish Latin America, and the Philippines

Background reference digest. Crawled 2026-07-13 in Portuguese and Spanish. Reliability tags:
[documented] = primary/institutional source or dated news with named source · [news-reported] = press claim without named methodology · [devotional-claim] = church/devotional site tradition · [practitioner-consensus] = repeated across many devotional/commercial sources.

**Crowd-number caution:** a prior "hundreds of thousands" claim failed verification. Every attendance figure below carries its exact source. The largest *verified-to-a-named-source* single-day figure found anywhere is **~70,000 (Buenos Aires, Balvanera, 19 Apr 2026, estimate attributed to the Archdiocese of Buenos Aires via La Nación)**. ## 1. BRAZIL

### 1.1 Scale and history of the devotion

- Brazil treats Expedito as **"o santo das causas justas e urgentes"** (the saint of just and urgent causes) — the standard epithet on virtually every Brazilian source. [practitioner-consensus]
- Portuguese Wikipedia: "No Brasil sua veneração ganhou corpo nos anos 80 e hoje ele tem multidões de devotos" (*In Brazil his veneration gained traction in the 1980s and today he has multitudes of devotees*); "sua imagem circula em chaveiros, cartazes, panfletos e santinhos distribuídos aos milhares" (*his image circulates on keychains, posters, pamphlets and prayer cards distributed by the thousands*). https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedito_de_Melitene [news-reported — Wikipedia summarizing press]
- Devotional/press summaries agree popularity intensified in the 20th c. with the "Novena das Causas Urgentes" and boomed from the 1980s. https://vidaefe.com.br/conheca-a-historia-e-devocao-de-santo-expedito-o-santo-das-causas-urgentes/ ; https://gazetapenhense.com.br/noticia/247/santo-expedito-o-santo-das-causas-urgentes [devotional-claim]
- He is invoked for: financial trouble, illness, exams, lawsuits, "negative forces," anything needing a fast answer; patron of soldiers, students, travelers, merchants. https://formacao.cancaonova.com/igreja/santos/conheca-a-historia-de-santidade-do-santo-das-causas-urgentes/ [devotional-claim]
- Two Brazilian municipalities are named after the saint: **Santo Expedito (SP)** and **Santo Expedito do Sul (RS)**. https://www.turismo.rs.gov.br/turismo/municipio/visualizar/342 [documented]
- On the devotional-services portal "Meu Santo" (reported by Gazeta do Povo), Santo Expedito **dominated petitions at 21% of all requests and led "graças alcançadas" at "35,8% dos pedidos atendidos"** (35.8% of answered requests); the site reported ~10,000 visitors/day, 50,000 on holy days. https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/vida-e-cidadania/e-possivel-fazer-novena-pagar-promessa-e-pedir-uma-graca-ao-santo-devoto-pela-internet-agzr3tkgylqx1bneiuiekkrbi/ [news-reported — single portal's internal stats, not national data]

### 1.2 Santuário Diocesano de Santo Expedito — town of Santo Expedito, SP (Diocese of Presidente Prudente)

The biggest purpose-built Expedito shrine found in Brazil.

- Origin: devotion began in the **1940s** when the family of Arnóbio Guimarães Tenório brought an image to the region; after a grace received, they built a wattle-and-daub (pau a pique) chapel — the town itself took the saint's name. https://www.diocesepresidenteprudente.com.br/noticias/devocao-a-santo-expedito-move-construcao-de-um-dos-maiores-santuarios-do-brasil/ [documented — diocesan history]
- New sanctuary: ~**10,000 m²** built area on Rod. Júlio Budiski (SP-501); construction began **2010** (parish erected 2009); **R$22 million** invested so far, ~R$8 million still needed; four annexed chapels. Diocese calls it "um dos maiores santuários do Brasil" (*one of the largest sanctuaries in Brazil*). Same diocesan URL. [documented]
- Feast crowds: regional news (Ifronteira, 2026): "Ao longo do final de semana, são esperados **mais de 30 mil romeiros** nos eventos em homenagem ao santo em 2026" (*over 30,000 pilgrims expected across the weekend*), 18–21 April, with masses, shows, and a **cavalgada** (horseback procession) to town center on 21 April; 2026 marked the **68th festa**. https://ifronteira.com/regiao/223/festa-de-santo-expedito-reune-milhares-de-fieis-em-missas-shows-e-cavalgada-confira-a-programacao [news-reported — organizer expectation, not counted attendance]
- A search-result snippet from local press put it at "mais de 50 mil devotos e romeiros durante os três dias" against a town population of ~2,800 — treat as upper-bound organizer claim. (via AASCJ/aascj.org.br search snippet) [devotional-claim]
- Monthly rhythm: "Todo mês, no dia 19, missas das 15h serão celebradas no Santuário" (*every month on the 19th, 3 pm masses*). https://www.imparcial.com.br/noticias/todo-mes-no-dia-19-missas-das-15h-serao-celebradas-no-santuario-de-santo-expedito,58573 [documented — local paper O Imparcial]
- Feast-day mass schedule: weekday 19 April = 15:00 + 19:30; weekend = 10:00/15:00/19:30, streamed on Facebook. https://santuariosantoexpedito.com.br/ [documented — shrine site]

### 1.3 São Paulo city — Capela (Militar) de Santo Expedito, Luz

- The famous SP-capital site is the **Capela Militar de Santo Expedito**, Rua Jorge Miranda 284/308, Luz district, attached to the ROTA (military police) barracks; **founded June 1942**. https://cronicasmacaenses.com/2016/07/05/capela-militar-de-santo-expedito-em-sao-paulo-o-santo-das-causas-justas-e-urgentes/ ; https://arquidiocesemilitar.org.br/capelania-militar-santo-expedito-da-policia-militar-de-sao-paulo.html [documented]
- Practice: mass every 19th of the month including a **midnight mass on the 19th** ("missa à meia-noite"); overnight queues reported by visitors; capacity-limited entry (e.g., 60 people per Sunday mass in pandemic-era listings). https://www.horariodemissa.com.br/igreja.php?k=PYZXg ; https://www.capelamilitarsantoexpedito.com.br/missas/ [documented for schedule; queue reports are visitor accounts — practitioner-consensus]

### 1.5 The "dia 19" rhythm

- April 19 is the feast; **the 19th of every month** is a devotional day nationwide (and across Latin America): monthly votive masses at Presidente Prudente sanctuary, midnight mass at the Luz chapel, monthly gatherings in Fortaleza (Arquidiocese de Fortaleza: "todo dia 19 de cada mês, dezenas de fiéis se reúnem"). https://www.arquidiocesedefortaleza.org.br/paroquia-nossa-senhora-aparecida-no-montese-realiza-1a-festa-de-santo-expedito/ [documented]
- No special "month of Santo Expedito" found; the pattern is **annual 19 April + monthly 19th**. [practitioner-consensus]

### 1.6 Prayer culture: milheiro, corrente, public thank-yous

This is the distinctive Brazilian practice cluster:

- **The promessa do milheiro**: devotees promise that, if the grace is granted, they will "mandar imprimir um milheiro" — have **1,000 prayer cards (santinhos) printed** and distribute them (left in churches, squares, phone booths, buses). Print shops sell "Milheiro de Santinho Santo Expedito — Oração de Promessa — 1000 unidades" as a stock product: https://www.santinhosunidospelafe.com.br/santinhos/santo-expedito-milheiro-de-santinhos-e-oracao-para-promessa ; https://www.graficauirapuru.com.br/produto/milheiro-de-santinho-santo-expedito-oracao-de-promessa-1000 ; https://www.personalizefacil.com.br/milheiro-de-santinho-santo-expedito-oracao-de-promessa-1000-un ; https://mcmcopias.loja2.com.br/3883177-Santo-Expedito-1000-Milheiro [documented — the commerce IS the evidence]
- Standard card format: 7×10 cm, couchê brilho 90 g paper, image front / prayer back; some milheiro packs bundle **4 medals + 2 pocket images**. https://www.santinhosdobrasil.com.br/santinho-santo-expedito-7x10-cm [documented]
- The canonical promise prayer embeds the propagation clause — the devotee vows to "levar o seu nome a todos que têm fé" (*carry your name to all who have faith*) and to publicize the devotion; distributing the cards fulfills it. [practitioner-consensus]
- Online extension: the "Meu Santo" portal (from 2005) let devotees pay **R$20 to have 1,000 santinhos of the saint sent out**, and forwarded novena requests to 9,000 praying members; a priest (Pe. Alexander Cordeiro Lopes) endorsed it as "uma corrente de oração, o que é uma coisa boa" (*a prayer chain, which is a good thing*). Gazeta do Povo URL above. [news-reported]
- Human-interest example of lifelong gratitude promessa: https://www.campograndenews.com.br/brasil/cidades/santo-expedito-atendeu-pedido-em-7-meses-e-ganhou-gratidao-eterna-de-tereza [news-reported]
- Classified thank-you ads ("Agradeço a Santo Expedito por graça alcançada" in newspaper classifieds) are widely described as the pre-internet form of the same vow, but **no direct newspaper-archive example was captured this crawl** — flag for follow-up (Folha/O Globo classified archives). [practitioner-consensus — unverified this session]

## 2. ARGENTINA — the largest verified crowds anywhere

### 2.1 Santuario de San Expedito, Balvanera, Buenos Aires

- Site: the historic **Parroquia Nuestra Señora de Balvanera, Bartolomé Mitre 2411** (Once/Balvanera, founded 1833); functions as the city's San Expedito santuario, established as such ~22 years before 2026 (≈2004). https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sociedad/donde-queda-el-santuario-de-san-expedito-en-buenos-aires-para-rezar-pedir-y-agradecer-nid16042026/ ; https://parroquiadelcarmenvcp.com.ar/iglesia/santuario-de-san-expedito/ [documented]
- **19 April 2026**: La Nación reported lines "hasta 20 cuadras de fila" (*up to 20 blocks*), waits near 6 hours, and "cerca de **70.000 personas** pasaron y tocaron la imagen" (*about 70,000 people passed and touched the image*) — figure attributed to the **Archdiocese of Buenos Aires**; procession and mass led by Archbishop **Jorge García Cuerva** ("Que con la paz nos cure de la intolerancia, de la falta de respeto"). Devotee refrain: "Es milagroso." Petitions centered on health, work, peace; gratitude signs on gates; San Expedito tattoos noted. https://www.lanacion.com.ar/sociedad/es-milagroso-una-multitud-de-fieles-hicieron-hasta-20-cuadras-de-fila-para-ingresar-al-santuario-de-nid19042026/ [news-reported with named source — BEST-SOURCED BIG NUMBER IN THIS FILE]
- Neighborhood-impact analysis (local blog, 24 Apr 2026): 60,000–80,000 range, "un recordatorio de la capacidad de convocatoria de los santuarios urbanos en tiempos de crisis" (*a reminder of urban shrines' convening power in times of crisis*); street-vendor displacement issues. https://comunasalsur.wordpress.com/2026/04/24/balvanera-epicentro-de-la-fe-el-fenomeno-san-expedito-y-su-impacto-en-la-dinamica-barrial/ [news-reported — blog]
- Post-pandemic return crowds 2022: https://www.infobae.com/sociedad/2022/04/19/una-multitud-de-fieles-volvio-a-celebrar-el-dia-de-san-expedito-de-manera-presencial-en-balvanera-tras-dos-anos-de-pandemia/ [news-reported]
- Church-establishment embrace: AICA (Argentine Catholic news agency) covers the "fiesta grande" at Balvanera. https://aica.org/noticia-fiesta-grande-de-san-expedito-en-el-santuario-de-balvanera [documented — official Catholic press]

### 2.2 Santuario de San Expedito, Bermejo (Caucete, San Juan) — desert pilgrimage

- Spanish Wikipedia: located in **Bermejo, depto. Caucete, San Juan**, ~100 km from San Juan city on RN 141; described as Argentina's only *santuario* to the saint (pre-Balvanera framing); origin: image brought from Buenos Aires "para ayudar a una lugareña" (*to help a local woman*) — **Petronila Mercado de Lucero**, whose husband's recovery from alcoholism sparked the cult; her home was the first chapel; formal construction from **1996**. "Se estima que en 2008 fueron **70.000** las personas que se acercaron al lugar" (*an estimated 70,000 people came in 2008*). https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santuario_de_San_Expedito [news-reported via Wikipedia; 70k figure uncited there — treat with care]
- Provincial government 2025: "más de **10 mil fieles** que año a año visitan el santuario" (*more than 10,000 faithful visit each year*) — note the order-of-magnitude gap vs the 2008 claim; 2025's April 19 fell on Holy Saturday so the program merged with Easter (Vía Crucis, vigil, processions 10:30 and 17:00); Ministry of Tourism served sopaipillas y mate cocido. https://sisanjuan.gob.ar/23-turismo-y-cultura/2025-04-11/61431-miles-de-fieles-llegaran-a-bermejo-para-honrar-a-san-expedito [documented — government source; USE THIS LOWER NUMBER AS FLOOR]

- Provincial bus program "RedTulum te lleva a San Expedito" (state lays on transport for the feast). https://sisanjuan.gob.ar/23-gobierno/2024-04-11/55314-redtulum-te-lleva-a-san-expedito [documented]
- Origin dating: Radio María Argentina podcast frames it as beginning "en los años 50 con la fe de una vecina, Petronila Lucero" (*in the 1950s with the faith of a local woman*). https://radiomaria.org.ar/programacion/senderos-de-mi-tierra/san-expedito-una-marea-de-fe-que-une-pueblos-desiertos-y-fronteras/ [devotional-claim]

- History: the venerated image was **recovered from storage in the Viña del Mar temple warehouses in 2000** — i.e., the mass phenomenon is essentially a 21st-century growth. https://parroquiaderenaca.cl/san-expedito/ [documented — parish account]
- Bishops'-conference news: "Miles de fieles en las Misas de San Expedito." https://www.iglesia.cl/2904-miles-de-fieles-en-las-misas-de-san-expedito.html [documented — official Catholic press]

## 8. OFFICIAL CHURCH POSITION (Vatican / bishops, PT+ES sources)

- **Not in the current Roman Martyrology**: he "não foi incluso na edição de 2001 do Martyrologium Romanum" (*was not included in the 2001 edition*). Spanish sources add that in **1906 Pope St. Pius X ordered Expeditus removed** from the martyrology and he was not re-inscribed in 2001. https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedito_de_Melitene ; https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedito ; 1908 pamphlet "La cuestión de San Expedito" digitized by Toledo city archive: https://www.toledo.es/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/1908_la-cuestion-de-san-expedito.pdf [documented for 2001 absence; the 1906 Pius X order is widely repeated but primary decree not sighted this crawl — verify before asserting]
- Status characterization from Spanish Catholic commentary: the cult is **"tolerated, not admitted"** (tolerado, no admitido), compared to the cult of Charlemagne; devotion permitted, sainthood not officially vouched. (via religionenlibertad.com "San Expedito. Sí, pero no." — article now 404, characterization from search index; also eltestigofiel.org hagiography) [news-reported — Catholic commentary]
- Yet the earliest layer is genuinely ancient: an Expeditus appears among six soldiers martyred at Melitene in the **Martyrologium Hieronymianum** (April 18/19). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus [documented]
- The famous debunking legend: in **1781 a box of unlabeled catacomb relics arrived at a Paris convent marked "Spedito"** (express shipping), and the nuns took it for the saint's name — a story itself of doubtful historicity (the 1781 date collides with his documented patronage of Acireale, Sicily, also 1781). https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expedito [news-reported — the legend is well attested as a legend, not as fact]
- **Institutional embrace in practice**: Vatican News (PT) carries him as santo do dia for 19 April (https://www.vaticannews.va/pt/santo-do-dia/04/19/s--expedito.html); Brazilian dioceses erect canonical parishes and a diocesan sanctuary in his name (Presidente Prudente, Campo Limpo, Santo Amaro, Santos, Guaxupé); the Archbishop of Buenos Aires personally leads the Balvanera feast; Argentina's AICA and Chile's iglesia.cl cover the feasts approvingly; Franciscanos.org.br and Canção Nova publish his hagiography. **No hostile or restrictive statement from CNBB or any Latin American bishops' conference was found** — the operative posture is full pastoral accommodation of a saint absent from the official martyrology. ## 9. CROSS-CUTTING NOTES FOR THE WIKI

- **Iconography is uniform across all countries**: Roman soldier, cross inscribed HODIE (today), foot crushing a crow/raven crying CRAS (tomorrow) — today-vs-tomorrow is the devotion's entire semantic engine, and maps perfectly to "urgent causes." [practitioner-consensus]
- **The monthly 19th** is the load-bearing recurring observance in Brazil, Chile, Argentina, Peru alike — an unusually strong "monthly mini-feast" pattern (cf. Brazilian dia 27 for N. Sra. do Perpétuo Socorro etc.).
- **Crowd-number ledger (all figures with sources above):** Buenos Aires Balvanera ~70,000 (Archdiocese est., 19 Apr 2026); Bermejo San Juan 70,000 (2008 claim, uncorroborated) vs 10,000+ (2025 government); Reñaca Chile ~30,000 (Emol 2007) and 15–20,000 monthly (parish priest); Santo Expedito SP >30,000 over 3–4 days (organizers 2026); Niterói Fonseca ~5,000 in a day (A Tribuna RJ). **Nothing verifies "hundreds of thousands" at any single site or day.**
- The economics — milheiro printing, votive candle SKUs at Canção Nova, estampita street vendors at Balvanera, R$30 pilgrim lunches at the SP sanctuary — are first-class parts of the practice, not accretions (same posture as our Lazada/amulet-market principle).
- Origin-story texture worth keeping: both flagship Latin American rural shrines began with **a woman's household grace** (Petronila Lucero's husband's alcoholism in Bermejo; the Tenório family's grace in Santo Expedito SP) — folk-founded, later regularized by dioceses.
