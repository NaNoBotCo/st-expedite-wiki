# Saint Expeditus — Iconography (raw background)

Crawled 2026-07-13. Companion to 01-hagiography-history.md (same reliability tags and source list).

---

## 1. The standard image type (the one everyone knows)

A YOUNG ROMAN SOLDIER (usually read as a centurion; sometimes "legionary" of Legio XII Fulminata), beardless, in armor, red cloak common in modern statues:
- RIGHT hand: raised CROSS bearing the Latin word HODIE ("today"). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- LEFT hand: PALM branch (martyr's palm). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://www.molossia.org/stexpeditus.html
- RIGHT foot: trampling/crushing a CROW (sometimes described as raven; one hoodoo source says the devil appeared "as a crow or a snake"). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://saint-expedite.blogspot.com/2014/12/introducing-st-expedite-of-melitene.html
- From the crow's mouth: a BANDEROLE (speech scroll) reading CRAS ("tomorrow"). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Some renderings put HODIE on a plaque/tablet rather than on the cross; anastpaul describes crucifix-in-right-hand + palm-in-left, crow underfoot. — https://anastpaul.com/2019/04/19/saint-of-the-day-19-april-st-expeditus-died-303-martyr/

## 2. The pun machinery (why a crow)

- Triple pun: (a) the saint's NAME (expeditus = unencumbered, quick); (b) HODIE vs CRAS = today vs tomorrow (anti-procrastination); (c) the CROW'S CALL — Romans onomatopoeized the crow's caw as "cras cras," so the bird literally says "tomorrow, tomorrow" in Latin. Crushing it = refusing delay. [documented as standard interpretation] — https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html ; search synthesis
- The legend the image illustrates: on the point of converting, the Devil appears as a crow croaking "cras"; Expeditus stamps on it declaring "Hodie! I will be a Christian TODAY," and is baptized at once. [legend — the image almost certainly generated/shaped this story, not vice versa; Delehaye's view is that the NAME generated the whole speed-devotion] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://www.catholic.org/saints/saint.php?saint_id=347 ; expanded quote in anastpaul: "Hodie, hodie — Today, today! I will not leave anything for tomorrow, starting now I will be Christian."
- Delehaye (via Wikipedia): "The name has provoked puns, so he has become the saint of rapidity" — i.e., iconography is downstream of folk etymology. [scholarly position] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus

## 3. Earlier / variant image types

- CLOCK TYPE (earlier): "Early images of Saint Expeditus show him as a soldier squashing a crow beneath his right foot and bearing a CLOCK"; the cross-with-HODIE is the LATER replacement of the clock. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus (early variants: clock instead of cross); https://santosepulcro.co.il/en/saints/st-expeditus/
- GERMAN 18TH-CENTURY TYPE: St. Expeditus POINTING TO A CLOCK, with NO crow at all — invoked against procrastination. Germany's clock-variant is the main regional alternative to the crow type. [documented] — search synthesis (multiple sources); https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html ("Germanic variant: depicts a clock instead of crow symbolism")
- TURIN TYPE (claimed medieval): an ELDERLY MAN WITH A WHITE BEARD near a CLOCK — completely different from the young-soldier type; associated with his role as patron of traders in medieval Turin. [devotional-claim — the medieval dating of Turin's cult is itself contested] — https://anastpaul.com/2019/04/19/saint-of-the-day-19-april-st-expeditus-died-303-martyr/ ("Turin/Medieval: elderly man with white beard near a clock")
- SICILIAN CHURCHES: early depictions appear in Italian (esp. Sicilian) churches from the LATE 17TH AND EARLY 18TH CENTURIES. [documented] — search synthesis; Acireale patronage 1781 (Thurston, The Month 108 (1905) 546, per Wikipedia footnote)

## 4. Dating the standard type

- Sequence supported by the sources: (1) late-17th/early-18th c. Sicilian church images + 18th-c. German clock prints = the cult's first big visual phase; (2) clock-and-crow compositions; (3) clock replaced by HODIE cross, CRAS banderole added — the now-standard type; (4) MASS DIFFUSION of the standard type in the LATE 19TH CENTURY, when (per Kuefler) the cult "expanded greatly" — i.e., the familiar chromolithograph/statue image crystallized as a 19th-century commercial devotional product. [scholarly-contested for exact dates; the 19th-c. crystallization matches Kuefler's abstract but needs his full text for precision] — Kuefler abstract https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9809.12408 ; sources above
- Kuefler calls it "his unforgettable image — a Roman soldier crushing a crow or raven underfoot" and tracks its ADOPTION by Italian fascists and Haitian vodou practitioners (20th c.). [documented] — Wiley abstract
- Art UK holds at least one painting catalogued as "Saint Expeditus" (not fetched): https://artuk.org/discover/artworks/saint-expeditus-126020 — lead for dated European canvases.

## 5. Regional color & material culture

- RÉUNION: shrines and statues in saturated BLOOD-RED — red-painted roadside oratories with candles, flowers, ex-votos, small statues. Red is the saint's color there absolutely; decapitated statues appear when petitions fail (punishment/coercion of the saint) — a striking iconoclastic practice attached to his image. Syncretic overlay with Kali (Tamil Réunionnais) maps onto the red. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://catholicism.org/patron-saint-for-the-impatient.html
- NEW ORLEANS / HOODOO: red again — red candles, red flowers; statue at Our Lady of Guadalupe Chapel is the cult image (crate-marked EXPEDITE per legend); offering vocabulary (pound cake, water glass) belongs to the image's altar setting; work with him traditionally on WEDNESDAYS with red candles (one line of practice says Thursdays/yellow). [documented practice] — https://saint-expedite.blogspot.com/2014/12/introducing-st-expedite-of-melitene.html ; https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html
- ITALY: standard young-soldier type; patron of Acireale (Sicily); merchants/traders association colors his prints toward commerce. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- BRAZIL/LATIN AMERICA: standard type on mass-market prayer cards/statues; enormous feast-day devotion (São Paulo). [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
