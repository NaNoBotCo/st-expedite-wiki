## 1. The name and the supposed person

- Name/identity: "Expeditus" is Latin for a soldier marching without pack / light-equipped infantryman ("free from encumbrances"), which itself suggests the entry may denote an anonymous soldier known only by military category rather than a personal name. [scholarly-contested] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://newdailycompass.com/en/saint-expeditus
- Alleged vitals in devotional media: born 261 in Regensburg, Raetia (Germany); died 303 at Melitene, Armenia Minor (modern Malatya, Turkey); beheaded in the Diocletianic Persecution. The Regensburg birth detail is uncited anywhere scholarly. [devotional-claim] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Rank: variously "Roman centurion" or "commander of the XII Legion." If a soldier stationed at Melitene ca. 300, he would plausibly have belonged to Legio XII Fulminata, the legion garrisoned there (cited to R. Knox M'Elderry, "The Legions of the Euphrates Frontier," Classical Quarterly 3 (1909): 44–53). The legion inference is conditional, not evidence he existed. [scholarly-contested] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Executing authority in devotional accounts: order of Caesar Galerius ("son of a pagan priestess"); betrayed by a jealous rival; scourged before his legion, then beheaded after refusing to recant. No ancient source. [legend] — https://anastpaul.com/2019/04/19/saint-of-the-day-19-april-st-expeditus-died-303-martyr/
- Some devotional accounts add: the group was converted and baptized by Saint Anthimus and condemned by the judge Marcian. Circulates via aggregator sites; no primary citation found. [devotional-claim] — via search results incl. https://grokipedia.com/page/Expeditus (unverified aggregator)
- Devotional tradition sometimes pairs him chronologically with St. Philomena (c. 290–302) as a contemporary. [devotional-claim] — https://newdailycompass.com/en/saint-expeditus
- Feast day: April 19. [documented] — all sources.
- Denominational status: venerated in Catholic and (per Wikipedia) Lutheran and Oriental Orthodox contexts; NOT in Eastern Orthodox liturgical books — "not mentioned in the Menaion, Prologue, or Synaxarion and therefore not officially recognized." [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Pre-Congregation "canonization" (i.e., never formally canonized; cult predates canonization procedure). [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus

## 2. Martyrology record

### Hieronymian Martyrology (Martyrologium Hieronymianum, first half of 5th c.; origin debated northern Italy 5th c. vs. Gaul 6th c.)
- April 19 entry (the six Martyrs of Melitene): "In Arminia Militana civitate Hermogeni, Gagi, Expediti, Aristonici, Rufi, Galatae una die coronatorum" — "In the city of Melitene in Armenia, Hermogenes, Caius (Gaius), Expeditus, Aristonicus, Rufus, and Galata(s), crowned [martyred] on the same day." [documented] — https://newdailycompass.com/en/saint-expeditus ; https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- CRITICAL DETAIL: the name Expeditus occurs in the Hieronymianum among groups of martyrs on BOTH April 18 and April 19 — assigned in one case to ROME and in the other to MELITENE. Per the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia (Herbert Thurston): "there is no vestige of any tradition which would corroborate either mention, whereas there is much to suggest that in both lists the introduction of the name is merely a copyist's blunder. Hundreds of similar blunders have been quite definitely proved to exist in the same document." [documented as scholarly position] — https://santosepulcro.co.il/en/saints/st-expeditus/ (reproducing Catholic Encyclopedia/Thurston text); corroborated in Butler's Lives of the Saints (Thurston & Attwater rev. ed.)
- Catholic Encyclopedia verdict: "There is no adequate reason to think that any such saint was ever invoked in the early Christian centuries; in fact it is more than doubtful whether the saint ever existed." [documented as scholarly position] — same sources.

### (Old) Roman Martyrology (Baronius line, 1583–1956 editions)
- April 19 entry: "At Melitene, in Armenia, the holy martyrs Hermogenes, Caius, Expeditus, Aristonicus, Rufus, and Galatas." So the group DID stand in the traditional Roman Martyrology, though with no feast on any calendar. [documented] — search-confirmed wording; see https://catholicsaints.info/book-of-saints-hermogenes-caius-expeditus-aristonicus-rufus-and-galatas/
- Book of Saints (Benedictine monks of Ramsgate, 1921), entry for the six: "We have otherwise no reliable information concerning them." [documented] — https://catholicsaints.info/book-of-saints-hermogenes-caius-expeditus-aristonicus-rufus-and-galatas/
- Wikipedia: he "was included in Italian martyrologies before 1781" and "appeared in the Acta Sanctorum volume for April in 1675" (brief mention only). [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://santosepulcro.co.il/en/saints/st-expeditus/

### Current status (post-Vatican II)
- He was never on the General Roman Calendar, so the oft-repeated "removed from the calendar in 1969" is imprecise; what circulates is that he was "purged from the calendar" post-Vatican II "alongside Saint Christopher and Saint Philomena." (Philomena's suppression was actually 1961; Christopher was demoted to local calendars in 1969 but kept in the Martyrology — the analogy is loose.) [scholarly-contested] — https://catholicism.org/patron-saint-for-the-impatient.html
- Claim circulating (Grokipedia and others): the revised Martyrologium Romanum (2001, ed. typica altera 2004) DROPPED the Expeditus entry because of historical doubts. NOT independently verified against the 2004 text in this crawl (archive.org copy exists: https://archive.org/details/MartRom2004 — check April 19 = XIII Kal. Maii). [scholarly-contested / verify] — via https://grokipedia.com/page/Expeditus (unreachable directly; text via search)
- Meanwhile Vatican News still carries a "St. Expeditus" saint-of-the-day page for April 19 (page exists but returned 403 to fetch). [documented existence] — https://www.vaticannews.va/en/saints/04/19/st--expeditus.html

## 3. Bollandist / scholarly skepticism

- Hippolyte Delehaye (Bollandist): "Expeditus" is a MISREADING OF "ELPIDIUS." Under this theory the entire saint is a scribal artifact; the evocative name then generated the cult ("the name has provoked puns, so he has become the saint of rapidity"). [documented as scholarly position] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://santosepulcro.co.il/en/saints/st-expeditus/ . Delehaye's general argument is in Les légendes hagiographiques; Wikipedia gives no precise page citation. 
- Herbert Thurston, S.J. — two relevant texts: (a) the Catholic Encyclopedia (1913) article quoted above; (b) "Flotsam and Jetsam," The Month, vol. 108 (December 1905), p. 546 — a contemporary piece (cited by Wikipedia for the Acireale patronage) written the very year of the Pius X controversy; likely the key contemporary skeptical/journalistic source on the 1905 affair. [documented] — Wikipedia footnote list via https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Expeditus&action=raw
- Butler's Lives of the Saints (Thurston–Attwater revision): "the earliest indication of devotion to St Expeditus comes only from the second half of the eighteenth century"; repeats the copyist-blunder analysis; treats the packing-case story as popular etiology. [documented as scholarly position] — via search snippets of Butler's text.
- Mathew Kuefler, "The Convertible Saint: Expeditus through Time and Space," Journal of Religious History 42.1 (March 2018): 25–51, doi:10.1111/1467-9809.12408. ABSTRACT (verbatim): "Expeditus, a Roman soldier who was for centuries simply a name on a list of early Christian martyrs, took on new life in the late eighteenth century in a Catholic cult that expanded greatly in the late nineteenth century and has continued to spread since then. His name linked him with time and the answering of prayers for urgent causes in particularly modern ways, despite growing scholarly scepticism about him. His unforgettable image — a Roman soldier crushing a crow or raven underfoot — has been adopted by Italian fascists and practitioners of Haitian vodou, among many others. Expeditus's popularity on the internet is only the latest version of this unusual devotion." [documented] — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9809.12408 ; author page https://history.sdsu.edu/people/kuefler . Kuefler is the single best modern scholarly treatment; Wikipedia leans on it for the Pius X claim. Full text paywalled; ResearchGate stub: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/311333873
- Kuefler's chronology (from abstract): name-on-a-list for centuries → cult born late 18th c. → GREAT EXPANSION late 19th c. → 20th c. appropriations (Italian fascists; Haitian vodou) → internet-era popularity. [documented]

## 4. Origin-of-the-cult legends (the crate stories) — all apocryphal

- The whole family of stories is an etiological urban legend: a shipped container marked with a dispatch word (Latin/Italian "spedito," French "expédit," English "expedite") is mistaken for the martyr's name. The same story is localized in Paris, São Paulo, New Orleans, and Haiti — "earmarks of an urban legend." [scholarly-contested → effectively debunked] — https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html
- Paris variant (dated 1781 in most tellings): a case of catacomb relics (Denfert-Rochereau catacombs per Wikipedia; "a body of a saint from the catacombs" per Butler) sent to a community of nuns in Paris, marked expédit / spedito; the nuns took it as the saint's name, prayed, were answered, propagated the cult. [legend] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; Butler's Lives via search; Craughwell, Arlington Catholic Herald, 11 Apr 2012 (Wikipedia's citation for this variant)
- New Orleans variant: crates of saint statues arrive at Our Lady of Guadalupe Chapel (church built 1826); one unlabeled statue's case is stamped EXPEDITE; the statue is so named and enshrined (it is still there, beside the church's Jude shrine). [legend] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://catholicism.org/patron-saint-for-the-impatient.html ; Zibart, Washington Post, 12 Sep 2005 (Wikipedia's citation)
- Réunion variant: "a mysterious parcel marked with expedit arrived as an aid to instill pious virtues"; another local version makes him a helper "in placing vengeful curses." [legend] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- KILLER COUNTER-FACT: 18th-century veneration in Germany and Sicily (and possibly medieval Turin) PREDATES the package story — so the crate legend cannot be the cult's real origin; at most it renamed/relaunched an existing devotion. [scholarly-contested but well-supported] — https://www.molossia.org/stexpeditus.html ; https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html ; Butler's Lives ("earliest indication ... second half of the eighteenth century")

## 5. Real documented cult history

- Turin, Italy: veneration possibly medieval — he was honored as patron of traders; a Turin district is said to be named after him. Turin iconography differed (see 02-iconography.md). [scholarly-contested] — https://anastpaul.com/2019/04/19/saint-of-the-day-19-april-st-expeditus-died-303-martyr/ ; https://newdailycompass.com/en/saint-expeditus ; https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html
- Acta Sanctorum, April volume, 1675: brief mention. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- 18th century: cult demonstrably popular in SICILY and GERMANY (German devotional prints invoked him against procrastination). [documented] — https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html ; search-corroborated
- 1781: Acireale, Sicily takes him as patron (Wikipedia cites Thurston 1905 for this); the same year Pius VI is said to have proclaimed him "protector of merchants and seafarers" — the Pius VI proclamation is cited only to a 2021 Daily Compass article, not to any 18th-c. document; treat with caution. [documented for Acireale / devotional-claim for Pius VI] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://newdailycompass.com/en/saint-expeditus
- Late 19th century: mass expansion of the cult (Kuefler). [documented]

## 6. Pope Pius X and 1905 — what exactly happened

- The claim as it universally circulates (Wikipedia wording): "His name was struck off the list of martyrs by Pope Pius X in 1905, and his image was banned from churches, without success." Wikipedia's support for this is Kuefler 2018, not a primary decree. [scholarly-contested — the fact of a 1905 intervention is well-attested, its precise instrument is not] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Secondary formulations found: "In 1905, Pope Pius X suppressed certain devotional practices associated with him and ordered the removal of his statues from some Roman churches due to concerns over superstition." No text of a Congregation of Rites decree, Index decree, or martyrology deletion from 1905 surfaced in this crawl. Note the OLD Roman Martyrology kept the six-martyr entry long after 1905 (it is in the 1956/1959 editions), which argues AGAINST a literal 1905 "martyrology deletion" and FOR a disciplinary action about images/devotions. [scholarly-contested — needs Kuefler full text or AAS 1905 to pin down] — search synthesis; https://grokipedia.com/page/Expeditus (unverified)
- Contemporary witness to check: Thurston, "Flotsam and Jetsam," The Month 108 (Dec 1905) 546 — same year, same author network (English Jesuit, Bollandist-aligned). [documented lead]
- Outcome: the intervention failed; devotion continued and grew ("without success"). [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus

## 7. Modern devotion (for context; commerce/practice detail beyond scope here)

- Patronages: emergencies, urgent causes, expeditious solutions, against procrastination, merchants, navigators/sailors, revolutionaries, dispatchers, examinations/students, settlement of prolonged legal cases; modern folk-extension to programmers/hackers/computer people. [devotional-claim] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://www.catholic.org/saints/saint.php?saint_id=347 ; https://www.jesterbear.com/Aradia/SantEspedito.html
- Brazil: April 19 feast crowds in São Paulo "reaching in the hundreds of thousands." [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Réunion: ubiquitous BRIGHT RED roadside altars/oratories with ex-votos, candles, flowers, statuettes; syncretized with the goddess Kali among Tamil Réunionnais (Suryanarayan, Eurasia Review, 12 Oct 2018); devotion "officially considered taboo but widespread"; statues found DECAPITATED when requests go unfulfilled (or, per other readings, to coerce/punish the saint); also invoked for vengeful curses. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Chile: image brought to Viña del Mar by a devotee; parish approval story ("in less than nine days"); popular pilgrimage shrine in Reñaca. [devotional-claim] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus (citing Craughwell 2012)
- New Orleans: statue at Our Lady of Guadalupe Chapel (International Shrine of St. Jude); veneration braided into Louisiana Creole folklore and hoodoo — amulets, flowers, red candles, intercessory prayers; classic offering = POUND CAKE (Sara Lee brand traditional; homemade acceptable) + glass of water + red flowers, typically AFTER the favor (payment-on-delivery logic); obligation to "spread his name" / publish thanks (newspaper personal ads, parish bulletin); nickname "the Minute Saint," names Expedite/Expedito/Spedito/Espidee/"Speedy"; Hyatt's Hoodoo–Rootwork–Witchcraft–Conjuration documents mid-20th-c. practice; devotion also in Independence, Louisiana; Haiti (vodou); Argentina; Philippines. Chapel's own website notably does NOT mention the statue. [documented for practice; devotional-claim for details] — https://saint-expedite.blogspot.com/2014/12/introducing-st-expedite-of-melitene.html ; https://catholicism.org/patron-saint-for-the-impatient.html ; Alvarado, The Conjurer's Guide to St. Expedite (Creole Moon, 2014)
- Micronation curiosity: the Republic of Molossia (Nevada) claims him as national patron saint. [documented as claim] — https://www.molossia.org/stexpeditus.html
- Kuefler: image adopted by Italian fascists and Haitian vodou practitioners; internet-era devotion is the latest phase. [documented] — Wiley abstract.
