# Saint Expedite in Europe + Media/Culture — Background Digest

Crawled 2026-07-13. Gap-area sweep: Italy / Germany–Austria / France / Spain / media & music / literature & film / commerce / comparative scholarship. Reliability tags: [documented] (verifiable fact or well-sourced record), [scholarly-contested] (scholars disagree or evidence weak), [devotional-claim] (asserted by devotional sources, unverified), [legend] (traditional narrative, not history).

## 1. ITALY — Sant'Espedito

### 1.1 The core Italian record (santiebeati.it — standard Italian hagiographic reference)
- Only secure data: feast day **19 April** and place of death **Melitene** (modern Malatya, Turkey), from the **Martirologio Geronimiano** (Hieronymian Martyrology, first half of 5th c.), "il Martirologio Geronimiano, dal quale dipendono tutti i martirologi che fino al Romano hanno trasmesso il nome di Espedito" ("the Hieronymian Martyrology, on which depend all the martyrologies that down to the Roman one have transmitted the name of Espedito"). [documented] — https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050
- santiebeati explicitly rejects both (a) Delehaye's Elpidius-misreading thesis as "unconvincing" and (b) the package-label legend, since the name already appears in the ancient Geronimiano. [scholarly-contested] — https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050
- Sicilian cult documented from **mid-18th century**, especially Messina and Acireale; in **1781** proclaimed **secondary patron of Acireale**, venerated as "protettore dei mercanti e dei navigatori" ("protector of merchants and navigators"). [documented] — https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050
- Turin: medieval street named for him where he already patronized merchants (see 1.3). [scholarly-contested — startlingly early; single-tradition claim] — https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050

### 1.2 Acireale (Sicily) — the strongest Italian center
- Cult **imported to Acireale from Messina in the first half of the 18th century**, during Acireale's famous free-market trade fair ("fiera franca"); Messina cult may go back to the **17th century**. [documented, per Italian Catholic press summarizing early-20th-c. records] — https://parrocchiasoave.it/dove-si-trova-la-tomba-di-santespedito-scopri-la-sua-storia-e-il-culto-in-italia/ ; https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/
- **18 April 1781**: proclaimed secondary patron of Acireale — sanexpedito.it attributes the act to **Bishop Corrado Deodati**; other Italian sources say the proclamation was under **Pope Pius VI** and add patronage of "merchants and navigators." [documented date; attribution varies between sources] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/ ; https://www.lalucedimaria.it/oggi-19-aprile-sant-espedito-santo-che-si-invoca-per-cause-urgenti/
- Statue by sculptor **Ignazio Castorina (1737–1822)** venerated in the small **Chiesa di Gesù e Maria**, Acireale. [documented] — https://www.nuoveedizionibohemien.it/index.php/il-culto-di-s-espedito-ad-acireale/ ; https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/
- Early 1800s: "la festa di S.Espedito veniva celebrata con pompe esterne di luminarie e di artifici pirotecnici" ("the feast of S. Espedito was celebrated with external displays of illuminations and fireworks"). [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/
- Three copper engravings document him as **"negotiorum et expeditionum patronus"** ("patron of business dealings and expeditions"). [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/
- Decline: local historian Prof. **Alfonso Sciacca** — "Il culto di S. Espedito era particolarmente vivace e sentito ad Acireale alla fine del XVIII sec." ("The cult of S. Espedito was particularly lively and deeply felt in Acireale at the end of the 18th century"); bishops later discouraged it because believers sought "favore di intervenire nei loro traffici" ("the favor of intervention in their business dealings") — i.e., too mercantile, mixing sacred and secular. [documented] — https://www.nuoveedizionibohemien.it/index.php/il-culto-di-s-espedito-ad-acireale/
- Feast still celebrated every 19 April with processions and prayers; Acireale routinely described as the center of the Italian devotion. [documented] — https://parrocchiasoave.it/dove-si-trova-la-tomba-di-santespedito-scopri-la-sua-storia-e-il-culto-in-italia/

### 1.3 Turin (Torino) — the "medieval merchants' patron" claim
- Claim: before **1437**, Turin's Via Garibaldi was called **"contrada di Sant'Espedito"**, and the saint was already invoked as merchants' patron — this would be the earliest cult trace anywhere, pre-dating the whole 18th-century-origin thesis. [scholarly-contested — repeated by santiebeati and devotional sites; if the street name is archivally real it is a major datum, but the identification with *this* Expeditus is uncertain] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/ ; https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050
- Marble statue by sculptor **Cattaneo (1924)** on the facade of the **Church of San Rocco**, Turin, plus another statue inside; **Church of S. Alfonso (1896)** contains an (incomplete) chapel dedicated to him. [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/
- Note: no Italian source found supports a "1781 Turin legend variant" — the 1781 date in Italy is the Acireale patronage proclamation, a genuinely documented event that happens to coincide with the year assigned to the (unrelated, legendary) Paris package story. This coincidence is worth flagging in the wiki. [documented coincidence]

### 1.4 Naples & Campania
- Naples cult developed in the **19th century**; a church at **Piazza Banchi Nuovi no. 5** venerated him a century ago; devotional booklet published (1905) "out of devotion by a Dominican nun from the monastery of S. Giovanni on Via Costantinopoli." [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/ ; https://agenziastampaitalia.it/speciali-asi/canzone-napoletana-nel-mondo/56777-san-espedito
- Current Naples sites: statue in **Church of S. Alfonso Maria de' Liguori (Marianella)**; monastery of **S. Giovanni, Via Costantinopoli**; **San Giovanni a Teduccio**; **Torre Annunziata** — former Church of S. Espedito "Trinità" (demolished for a street opening). [documented] — https://agenziastampaitalia.it/speciali-asi/canzone-napoletana-nel-mondo/56777-san-espedito ; https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/
- **Castellammare di Stabia**: wooden statue (1902) by **Raffaele Della Campa** in Church of Santa Maria della Pace; feast first celebrated 19 April 1904 with silver ornaments. **Marigliano**: altar dedicated 1909 in Church of Santa Maria del Suffragio. [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/ ; https://www.liberoricercatore.it/santespedito-martire-parrocchia-santa-maria-pace/
- Neapolitan practice: the **"novena of nine hours"** or **"flying novena"** (novena volante), especially by students before exams. [documented practice] — https://parrocchiasoave.it/dove-si-trova-la-tomba-di-santespedito-scopri-la-sua-storia-e-il-culto-in-italia/

### 1.5 Palermo and the rest of Sicily
- **Palermo**: cult spread mid-19th century; approved by **Cardinal Michele Celesia ("Ceresia") 29 December 1899**; a **parish church of Sant'Espedito built 1935 in via Nicolò Garzilli** — statue and an *unverified relic* preserved. Still an active parish (Facebook presence). [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/ ; https://www.facebook.com/santespeditopalermo/ ; https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g187890-d6279866-Reviews-Chiesa_di_Sant_Espedito-Palermo_Province_of_Palermo_Sicily.html
- **Giarre (CT)**: annual solemn Mass + procession 19 April at Oratory of S. Filippo Neri. **Milazzo (ME)**: polychrome statue, third altar, Church of S. Maria Maggiore (17th c. church). [documented] — https://www.sanexpedito.it/san-expedito-in-italia/

## 2. GERMANY & AUSTRIA — Heiliger Expeditus / Expedit

### 2.1 Strength and geography of the German-language cult
- Leading scholar: Austrian folklorist **Elfriede Grabner** — Expeditus was "vor allem im 19. und frühen 20. Jahrhundert zu den beliebtesten Volks- und Schutzheiligen" ("especially in the 19th and early 20th centuries among the most beloved folk and protective saints"). Cult peaked **c. 1870s–1920s**; **Vienna was "ein frühes Zentrum"** (an early center) from which devotion radiated to southern Germany, France, Italy, Spain and the Americas. [documented scholarship] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
- German nickname: **"der eilige Heilige"** — "the hasty/hurrying saint" (pun: eilig/heilig). [documented] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige ; https://www.domradio.de/nachrichten/2010-04-18/am-19-april-haette-der-eilige-heilige-seinen-namenstag
- fr.wikipedia claims the cult existed in Germany by the **17th century under the name "Elpide"/"Ilpide"** — consistent with the Elpidius identification. [scholarly-contested] — https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exp%C3%A9dit_de_M%C3%A9lit%C3%A8ne
- heiligenlexikon.de: "Tatsächlich geht Expedits Verehrung … höchstens bis ins 18. Jahrhundert zurück" ("In reality, Expedit's veneration goes back at most to the 18th century"). [scholarly-contested vs. the Turin/17th-c. claims] — https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html

### 2.2 Surviving images / shrines
- **Graz, Franziskanerkirche (Styria)**: late-19th-c. wooden statue of Expeditus as legionnaire with sword, martyr's palm and raven; **votive tablets still visible in 1979**, and prayer cards were still being distributed there in 1979. This is the flagship surviving German-language shrine. [documented] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
- Diocese of Augsburg maintains an official saint-of-the-day page for EXPEDITUS — residual mainstream recognition in Bavaria. [documented] — https://bistum-augsburg.de/Heilige-des-Tages/Heilige/EXPEDITUS
- German religious personal names: **Father Expeditus Kleber** (Capuchin, Türkheim youth chaplain, published a Rome pilgrimage account 1925); **Father Dr. Expeditus Schmidt (1868–1939)**, Franciscan of Dettelbach near Würzburg, writer/literary historian — evidence the name itself was in German religious use. [documented] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
- German-region iconographic peculiarity: Expeditus shown **with a clock** (elsewhere: cross inscribed *hodie*); Austria-Forum adds a variant with a **sundial showing 11 o'clock** ("eleventh hour", tied to the midday demon of acedia/sloth). [documented iconography] — https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050 ; https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige

### 2.3 Why it declined
- **1905 Vatican intervention** under Pius X: the cult had grown from "etymologischen und ikonographischen … und nicht aus glaubhaften Umständen" ("etymological and iconographic [motives] … and not from credible circumstances"). Grabner: the cult "wohl durch das Einschreiten der kirchlichen Stellen … allmählich verebbt" ("probably ebbed away gradually through the intervention of church authorities"). [documented] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
- Contemporary polemic: a **1904 theological journal** complained that modern speed-culture had invaded folk piety — "Telegraph und Eisenbahnen fungieren nicht mehr schnell genug" ("telegraph and railways no longer function fast enough [so people turn to the saint]"). [documented] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
- Modernity thesis (Austria-Forum): no coincidence that the hurrying saint boomed exactly as **railways, telegraph, telephone and ocean steamers** were transforming life; he reads as a saint *of* acceleration. [scholarly interpretation] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
- Precision on the "struck from the calendar" claim: he was never formally canonized and remained in the printed **Martyrologium Romanum (1956/1962 editions)**; what happened in 1905/1906 was a curial intervention against the cult's excesses and new images, widely reported as "Pius X struck his name / banned his image — without success." [scholarly-contested; sources disagree on exact juridical act] — https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- German legend variants collected: the Paris/New Orleans package-label story ("Espedito" read as a name); a claimed **Regensburg birth in 261** circulating in German hagiography. [legend] — https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- The 12th Legion "Fulminata" (thunderbolt legion) rain-miracle backstory and the companions' names (Hermogenes, Caius, Aristonicus, Rufus, Galatas) as told in German devotional sources. [legend] — https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html

## 3. FRANCE — Saint Expédit

### 3.1 The 1781 Paris legend and its criticism
- Standard telling: in **1781** a case containing relics of a saint from the **Denfert-Rochereau catacombs of Paris** was delivered to a Paris convent; the senders had written **"expédit"** on the case for fast delivery; the nuns took "Expédit" for the martyr's name and prayed to him; answered prayers spread the cult through France. [legend — the single most-repeated origin story] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus
- Killer objection: the name Expeditus appears in martyrologies **centuries before 1781** (Hieronymian, then Italian cult), so the label story cannot be the origin. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus ; https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050
- Aleteia (French Catholic media) goes further: the package story was **invented by anticlerical critics as satire** to discredit the cult; it argues the martyr probably existed as **Elpidius**, and that the name arose from a copying error — "un moine recopiant le martyrologe a accidentellement remplacé Elpidius … par Expeditus" ("a monk recopying the martyrology accidentally replaced Elpidius … with Expeditus"); the record's very terseness "plaide plutôt en faveur de la véracité de l'histoire" ("argues rather in favor of the story's truthfulness"). [scholarly-contested — Delehaye's Elpidius thesis, itself rejected by santiebeati] — https://fr.aleteia.org/2022/04/18/saint-expedit-a-t-il-existe/
- **Hippolyte Delehaye** (Bollandist) is the scholarly anchor: "Expeditus" possibly a scribal misreading of "Elpidius"; his prescient 1906 question, "Pourquoi, du groupe de martyrs … a-t-on détaché le seul Expédit ?" ("Why, out of the group of martyrs, was Expeditus alone singled out?") — answer: the etymology itself generated the devotion. [documented scholarship] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expeditus

### 3.2 Metropolitan France today
- French Wikipedia maintains an entire inventory article, "Représentations de saint Expédit," with **16+ regional sections** of church statues, e.g. **Bordeaux, église Saint-Pierre (with ex-votos)**, **Toulouse, basilique Notre-Dame de la Daurade**, **Brain-sur-Longuenée (église Saint-Didier)** — plus Belgium (Liège, Brussels, Bièvre, Dison, Becco, Boussoit), Portugal (Lisbon, Porto), Canada (Québec), Algeria (Kouba). Standard iconography: "généralement représenté, en couleurs vives, avec la chlamyde des soldats romains, tenant dans la main gauche la palme du martyr, et dans celle de droite une croix" ("generally depicted, in bright colors, with the Roman soldier's chlamys, holding in the left hand the martyr's palm and in the right a cross") inscribed HODIE, raven croaking CRAS CRAS. [documented] — https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repr%C3%A9sentations_de_saint_Exp%C3%A9dit
- **Marseille, église Saint-Cannat (Dominican church)**: notable French center of the devotion — and the hinge to Réunion (below). [documented] — https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html ; https://www.il-etait-une-fois-mes-ancetres-a-l-ile-bourbon.fr/l/le-culte-de-saint-expedit-a-lile-de-la-reunion/
- French Church directory nominis.cef.fr still lists him; French devotion described as "très répandu" (very widespread) at the popular level despite official coolness. [documented] — https://nominis.cef.fr/contenus/saint/6623/Saint-Expedit.html ; https://fr.aleteia.org/2022/04/18/saint-expedit-a-t-il-existe/

### 3.3 France → Réunion (colonial transmission)
- Founding episode as told on Réunion: **Fany Fleurié (later Chatel)**, stranded at the port of **Marseille at the end of WWI**, prayed before the Expédit statue in **Saint-Cannat**, vowing to establish his cult on Réunion if he got her home fast; she sailed **three days later**. She donated a statue c. **1923–25**; the **first statue was blessed on 3 May 1931 in the église Notre-Dame de la Délivrance, Saint-Denis** (Réunion). [documented local history, with legendary coloring] — https://www.il-etait-une-fois-mes-ancetres-a-l-ile-bourbon.fr/l/le-culte-de-saint-expedit-a-lile-de-la-reunion/ ; https://visites-guidees.net/le-culte-de-saint-expedit-a-la-reunion/ ; https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en
- Growth counts (Gauthier, scholarly): **1930s first chapels; 94 oratories in 1970; 337 in 1997** (last full count); popular sources say ~350–400 today. Shrines are bright red roadside oratories; **"28 % sont décapitées"** ("28% [of the statues] are decapitated") — beheading a statue punishes non-delivery or redirects sorcery. [documented scholarship] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en
- Gauthier: "le culte s'est « créolisé »" ("the cult became creolized") — red color linked to **Kali** worship brought by Indian indentured laborers post-1848 abolition; Tamil prayers, meat abstinence; statues never kept indoors ("too dangerous"); relationship transactional — "C'est donnant donnant" ("strictly give-and-take"). Hindus associate him with Vishnu; Muslim Indians treat shrines like Sufi sanctuaries. [documented scholarship + heiligenlexikon] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en ; https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html
- Full scholarly citation: **Nancy Gauthier, "Mémoire et Histoire : le culte de Saint Expédit à la Réunion," in *L'usage du passé entre Antiquité tardive et Haut Moyen Âge* (Presses universitaires de Rennes, 2008), pp. 133–145.** [documented] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en
- Réunion historian **Prosper Ève** is the local authority (interviewed with Bishop **Gilbert Aubry** in the KTO documentary, §6). — https://www.film-documentaire.fr/4DACTION/w_fiche_film/36864_1

## 4. SPAIN — San Expedito

- Devotion described as widespread in Spain "especially in **Seville and Madrid**." In Seville his image is in the **Iglesia del Santo Ángel** (Church of the Holy Angel), "in a discrete spot … at the feet of the left nave." [devotional-claim, multiple Spanish sources] — https://www.pildorasdefe.net/santos/celebraciones/santoral-catolico-san-expedito-19-abril ; http://sevilla-justa.blogspot.com/2008/06/san-expedito-abogado-de-los-casos.html
- Spanish framing: **"abogado de las causas urgentes"** ("advocate/lawyer of urgent causes") — legalistic title common in Spanish devotion; invoked for lawsuits and trials. [documented usage] — https://www.pildorasdefe.net/santos/celebraciones/santoral-catolico-san-expedito-19-abril ; https://es.catholic.net/op/articulos/34972/expedito-santo.html
- fr.wikipedia: in Madrid and Seville associated with Holy Week and All Saints; traditional fried pastries called **"huesos de san Expedito"** ("St. Expeditus' bones"). [devotional-claim — single-source, verify before wiki use] — https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exp%C3%A9dit_de_M%C3%A9lit%C3%A8ne
- Spain is clearly the thinnest of the four countries — no major shrine church surfaced; the New Orleans legend even claims the mislabeled statues were ordered *from Spain* (§6.1), implying Spanish workshops produced his imagery for export. [legend] — https://www.luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html
- Spanish-language devotion is far stronger in Latin America (Argentina, Chile, Brazil; São Paulo's 19 April feast drew ~200,000 by 2004). [documented] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en ; https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/BiographienE/Expedit.html

### 5.2 New Orleans culture (context for media)
- The famous statue: **Our Lady of Guadalupe Church & International Shrine of St. Jude, 411 N. Rampart St.**, oldest surviving church building in New Orleans, across from St. Louis Cemetery No. 1; claimed to be "the only [statue] of its kind in North America." Note the poetry: the urgent-causes saint is housed in the shrine of the *hopeless*-causes saint. [documented] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Guadalupe_Church_%26_International_Shrine_of_St._Jude ; https://www.nola.com/300/the-almost-true-legend-of-st-expedite-in-new-orleans/article_46709902-d3d3-504b-b501-c8d35b679e97.html
- NOLA legend: priests ordered a Virgin statue (from Spain); **two crates** arrived, the second marked **"EXPEDITE"** containing a Roman soldier — enshrined under that name. Lucky Mojo calls it "apocryphal": "there is no reason to think that the sculptors in Spain would have created such a figure unless asked to do so." [legend] — https://www.luckymojo.com/saintexpedite.html
- Hoodoo offerings: **pound cake** (informant tradition: specifically **Sara Lee** brand), red flowers, glass of water; "Yo' must give him flowers because if not, then someone out of the house will pass on" (New Orleans informant). ## 8. COMPARATIVE SCHOLARSHIP — urgent-causes saints & "popular canonization"

- Standard grouping: Expeditus (urgent causes) alongside **St. Jude Thaddeus** (hopeless/desperate causes) and **St. Rita of Cascia** (impossible causes). Italian formula: Expeditus is the saint "delle cause urgenti per antonomasia" ("of urgent causes par excellence"). [documented] — https://www.santiebeati.it/dettaglio/50050 ; https://newdailycompass.com/en/saint-expeditus-1
- **Name-driven patronage is the shared mechanism**: Jude's "lost causes" role is medieval, not ancient — he was avoided because his name evoked Judas Iscariot, so only the desperate prayed to him; Expeditus' entire profile flows from the Latin meaning of his (possibly miscopied) name. Delehaye's 1906 question (§3.1) makes exactly this point. Rita's "impossible" patronage likewise grew from her legend, not an early cult. [documented scholarship] — https://www.theeasternchurch.com/saints/saint-jude-complete-biography ; https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en
- **"Popular canonization" / manufactured saints**: Expeditus is the textbook case of a cult generated by etymology + iconography rather than a vita — the Vatican's own 1905 diagnosis ("etymological and iconographic … not credible circumstances," §2.3). He was never formally canonized; his sainthood is entirely *vox populi*, later merely tolerated. Compare the catacomb-saint trade (his legend even claims Denfert-Rochereau catacomb relics). [documented scholarship] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige ; https://www.il-etait-une-fois-mes-ancetres-a-l-ile-bourbon.fr/l/le-culte-de-saint-expedit-a-lile-de-la-reunion/
- **Gauthier's memory-instrumentalization thesis**: the cult "adapts Saint Expédit to contemporary needs rather than preserving historical accuracy"; Réunionnais preferred the fake-reliquary story over the Roman-legionary history because a mislabeled Vatican parcel was culturally nearer than imperial Armenia. Consumer-society coda: the cult mirrors the motto "tout avoir, tout de suite" ("have everything, right away"). [documented scholarship] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en
- **Creole-diaspora comparison** (Gauthier): Guadeloupe — top-ranked saint; **Haiti — invoked to eliminate enemies, his image turned upside-down during the prayer**; Brazil — explosive growth since 1997. The same saint is white-magic in Bologna, gray-market in Réunion, and openly aggressive in Haiti — a live demonstration that "orthodoxy" is a projection of local practice, not a property of the figure. [documented scholarship] — https://books.openedition.org/pur/4689?lang=en
- Modernity/acceleration reading (Austria-Forum + Gauthier converge): his 19th–20th-c. boom tracks railway/telegraph time-discipline; his 21st-c. survival tracks instant-gratification culture; "even in the Internet age, Expeditus appears as an appropriate and modern patron." [scholarly interpretation] — https://austria-forum.org/af/Wissenssammlungen/ABC_zur_Volkskunde_%C3%96sterreichs/Expeditus%2C_hl./Der_eilige_Heilige
