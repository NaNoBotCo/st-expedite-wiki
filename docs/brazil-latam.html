Brazil & Latin America

The largest living devotion on earth — 70,000-strong feast-day crowds, a town named for the saint, and the vow of a thousand prayer cards.

Reliability: practitioner-consensus. True as a description of what devotees and practitioners do.

If Réunion has the strangest Expedite and New Orleans the most photographed, Latin America has simply the biggest — this is where the living, mass devotion is loudest.

The largest crowds are in Buenos Aires

Documented — The single largest verified gathering is not in Brazil but in Argentina: the Santuario San Expedito at the Balvanera parish in Buenos Aires draws roughly 70,000 people on 19 April, who queue up to twenty blocks and wait hours to touch the image (Archdiocese estimate, via La Nación; the 2026 feast was led by Archbishop García Cuerva). The much-repeated "hundreds of thousands" claim fails everywhere — the verified ceiling is about 70,000 in a day.

Documented — Rural Argentina has its own centre at Bermejo (Caucete, San Juan), a village of ~700 that hosts tens of thousands of pilgrims yearly, its sanctuary founded in the 1950s from a household grace.

Brazil — a town named for the saint

Documented — Brazil's flagship is the Santuário Diocesano de Santo Expedito in the town of Santo Expedito, São Paulo — a municipality literally named after him, grown from a 1940s family chapel, now a ~10,000 m² sanctuary drawing tens of thousands over the feast weekend with a cavalgada (horseback procession). In São Paulo city, the historic site is the Capela Militar de Santo Expedito at the ROTA police barracks (founded 1942), with a midnight Mass each 19th; Rio venerates his image at the Igreja da Lapa dos Mercadores.

The rhythm: the 19th of every month

Practitioner — The observance is not only 19 April. Across Latin America the devotion runs on the 19th of every month — Chile's Reñaca parish has reported 15,000–20,000 worshippers monthly, and ~30,000 on a February 19th. This monthly pulse is a devotional fact worth watching for in the search data: a monthly sawtooth would confirm it from an entirely independent source.

A distinctive vow: the thousand prayer cards

Practitioner — Brazil's signature practice is the promessa do milheiro — a vow to have 1,000 santinhos (small prayer cards, ~7×10 cm) printed and scattered or handed out once your request is granted. It is enough of a fixture that print shops stock it as a product (~R$20 per thousand) and online portals exist purely to fulfil the promise on a petitioner's behalf.

The Philippines

Documented — Beyond Latin America, a Philippine devotion is attributed to 18th-century Franciscans, "from Aparri to Jolo," present in a dozen-plus named parishes (Parañaque, Las Piñas, Malate, Quezon City, the Naga basilica) as side-altar images rather than titular shrines, with Tagalog novenas in circulation.

Gap — Peru and Mexico remain thinly sourced here — Mexican devotion appears diffuse and home-altar based, with no single shrine yet documented. See How we know.
